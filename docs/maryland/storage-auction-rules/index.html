Maryland Storage Unit Auction Rules (Com. Law §§ 18-501 to 18-506): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Maryland storage auction rules: 60+ days default, 14-day demand, 10-day sale notice, 3-day ad, late fee cap, redemption and the 1-year surplus rule.

Quick answer

  • The operator may sell your stored property only after you've been in default for more than 60 days, at a public sale, for cash Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • Before the sale, you must get a notice of default demanding payment within a time not less than 14 days after it was mailed Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • At least 10 days before the sale you must be told its time, place and terms, and the sale must be advertised at least 3 days before (newspaper, email or online website) Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • You can redeem your property at any time before the sale by paying the amount needed to satisfy the lien Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • Late fees can't be more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of monthly rent, and only if the amount and timing are disclosed in your rental agreement Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • Leftover sale money is mailed to you by certified mail; if it comes back, the operator holds it 1 year, then it is presumed abandoned Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4] Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[6].

Maryland's self-storage law is in the Commercial Law Article, §§ 18-501 to 18-506 Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant, and the operator includes the owner, operator, lessor, sublessor, agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

The operator's lien

The operator has a lien on all personal property stored in your space for rent, labor, or other charges, and for expenses reasonably incurred in selling it Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[3]. Your rental agreement must say in bold type that the lien exists, that your property may be sold if you're in default, that a motor vehicle or watercraft may be towed or removed if you're in default for more than 60 days, and that a lien sale will be advertised in a newspaper, by email, or on an online website Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[3].

Default means failing to perform on time any obligation in the rental agreement Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1]. Until a lien sale, and unless the agreement says otherwise, care, custody and control of your stored property stays with you Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[5].

Late fees and lockouts

The operator may charge a reasonable late fee for each month rent isn't paid on time, but it may not be more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of the monthly rent. It can only be charged if the rental agreement discloses the amount of the fee and when it is charged Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

If you're in default, the operator may deny you access to your space Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

The subtitle does not set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Notice of default

Before any sale, the operator must notify you of the default by hand delivery, verified mail, or email at your last known address Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. Verified mail means any USPS or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1]. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your rental agreement, or one you later gave in a written change-of-address notice, so keep it current Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1].

The notice must include Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]:

  • a statement that the contents of your space are subject to the operator's lien
  • the operator's claim: charges due on the date of the notice, any additional charges that will come due before the sale, and when they come due
  • a demand for payment within a specified time, not less than 14 days after the notice was mailed
  • a statement that unless the claim is paid in time, your contents will be sold at a specified time and place
  • the name, street address and phone number of the operator or its agent you can contact

Email is allowed only if the rental agreement (or a written change to it) says in bold type that notice may be given by email and you initialed next to that statement. If the operator emails the default notice and gets no reply from your email address or delivery confirmation, it must send a second notice by verified mail to your last known postal address Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

Step 2: Notice of sale (at least 10 days before)

At least 10 days before the sale, the operator must tell you the time, place, and terms of the sale by hand delivery, verified mail, or email. If an emailed sale notice gets no reply or delivery confirmation at least 5 days before the sale, the operator must promptly send a second notice by verified mail Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

Step 3: Advertising (at least 3 days before)

At least 3 days before the sale, the operator must advertise its time, place and terms in a newspaper of general circulation where the sale will be held, by email, or on an online website Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

Step 4: The sale (after more than 60 days in default)

The operator may sell only if you've been in default for more than 60 days, and only at a public sale, for cash Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. The sale must be held at the facility where your property is stored; a sale on an online auction website counts as held at the facility Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights and other lienholders' rights. If the operator follows the subtitle, its liability to you is limited to the net sale proceeds Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

Vehicles and boats: if you've been in default more than 60 days and the stored property is a motor vehicle or watercraft, the operator may have it towed or removed instead of selling it. The operator isn't liable for damage after the towing company takes possession Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

How to stop the sale

At any time before the sale, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien and redeem your property Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The subtitle doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice of default Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

After a sale, the operator must pay the lien from the proceeds and mail any balance by certified mail to you or any other recorded lienholder at the last known address Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. If that mail comes back, the operator must hold the balance for 1 year after the sale for delivery on demand. After that year, the balance is presumed abandoned Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4] Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[6].

Unclaimed property in Maryland is handled by the Comptroller; search and claim at the official MD Unclaimed Property site Comptroller of Maryland[7]. Within that first year, contact the operator directly in writing.

Other Maryland rules

  • No living in units: the operator may not knowingly allow a space to be used as a residence, and you may not use it as one Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[2].
  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement limits the value of property you may store, that limit is deemed the maximum value of your stored property Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
  • Notices to the operator must go to the facility where your property is stored, by hand delivery or verified mail Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Maryland?
The operator can't sell until you've been in default for more than 60 days. Before then it must send a notice of default giving you at least 14 days after mailing to pay, notify you of the sale's time, place and terms at least 10 days before, and advertise the sale at least 3 days before Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
Can a Maryland storage facility lock me out?
Yes. If you are in default, the operator may deny you access to the leased space Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. Default means failing to perform on time any obligation in the rental agreement Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[1].
Can a Maryland storage facility send auction notices by email?
Only if the rental agreement (or a written change to it) says in bold type that notice may be given by email and you initialed next to that statement. If an emailed default notice gets no reply or delivery confirmation, the operator must send a second notice by verified mail. For an emailed sale notice, that applies if there's no reply or confirmation at least 5 days before the sale Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4].
How do I stop a storage auction in Maryland?
At any time before the sale, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien and redeem your property Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
What happens to leftover money after a Maryland storage auction?
The operator pays the lien from the proceeds and mails any balance by certified mail to you (or another recorded lienholder) at your last known address. If it's returned, the operator holds it for 1 year after the sale for delivery on demand; after that it is presumed abandoned Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4] Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[6]. Search Maryland's official unclaimed property site later Comptroller of Maryland[7].
Can they tow my car or boat from a Maryland storage unit?
Yes, if you've been in default more than 60 days and the stored property is a motor vehicle or watercraft, the operator may have it towed or removed instead of selling it Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[4]. Your rental agreement must warn you about this in bold type Maryland General Assembly (mgaleg.maryland.gov)[3].
