Virginia Storage Unit Auction Rules (Va. Code §§ 55.1-2900 to 55.1-2906): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Virginia storage auction rules: 10-day default, verified-mail notice with 20+ days to pay, online sales allowed, $250/$500 lien priority, and how to redeem.

Virginia's self-storage law is the Virginia Self-Service Storage Act, Code of Virginia Title 55.1, Chapter 29 (§§ 55.1-2900 to 55.1-2906) Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[1]. The sale process is in § 55.1-2902 Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. In Virginia law the renter is called the occupant, and "owner" includes the operator, lessor, sublessor, agent or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too, and the owner may use other legal remedies the Act doesn't displace Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[5].

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property stored in your leased space for rent, labor or other charges, and for expenses reasonably incurred selling it under this chapter. The lien attaches when the property is stored Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3].

While the property remains in the leased space, that lien is superior to other existing liens or security interests only up to $250, or $500 if the leased space is climate-controlled. The lien also extends to any sale proceeds left after perfected liens are paid, and the owner may keep those proceeds until the rest of its charges are paid Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3].

Your rental agreement must contain a statement, in bold type, telling you the lien exists and that property in the space may be sold to satisfy it if you're in default Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3]. Unless the agreement says otherwise, exclusive care, custody and control of what's in the space stays with you Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[7].

The owner of a self-service storage facility is not a warehouseman under Title 8.7 unless it issues a warehouse receipt, bill of lading or other document of title Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2].

Late fees and lockouts

Chapter 29 does not set a late-fee cap or a statutory late-fee formula, so check your rental agreement for any late charges Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[1].

Default means failing to perform on time any obligation in the rental agreement or this chapter Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2]. Whenever you're in default, the owner may deny you access to the leased space Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. There is no separate waiting period before lockout in § 55.1-2902.

The Act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[1].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: First default notice

If you're in default, the owner must notify you by regular mail at your last known address, or by electronic means if your rental agreement expressly allows it Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Your last known address is the street or email address in your rental agreement, or a later written change-of-address notice given the way the agreement requires Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2].

If the default isn't cured within 10 days after it occurs, the owner may proceed to enforce the lien by selling the contents of your unit at a public auction for cash Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Before that auction, it still has to send the further notice in Step 2.

Your agreement may let you name an alternative contact to receive the notices in this section. Refusing to name one doesn't change your rights, and that contact has no right to access the space or your property unless the agreement says so Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

Step 2: Further notice before the auction

After you've been in default for 10 days, and before the owner can sell your property under this chapter, it must send a further notice of default by verified mail, postage prepaid, to your last known address — or by electronic means if the agreement expressly allows it and the sender keeps proof of delivery (an electronic receipt, a fax confirmation, or a certificate of service) Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. "Verified mail" means any USPS or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2].

That further notice must include Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]:

  • an itemized statement of the owner's claim, with the charges due on the date of the notice and the date they became due
  • a demand for payment within a specified time not less than 20 days after the date of the notice
  • a statement that the contents of your leased space are subject to the owner's lien
  • a conspicuous statement that unless you pay in time, the contents will be sold at public auction at a specified time and place
  • the name, street address and telephone number of the owner or designated agent you can contact

A notice is presumed delivered when it's properly addressed and deposited with the Postal Service with postage prepaid, or when it's sent electronically with the required proof of delivery. In a dispute, the sender has the burden to show delivery Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

Other lienholders: if personal property has a fair market value over $1,000 and a creditor has filed a financing statement in your name (at the State Corporation Commission or in the locality where the facility is, or where your last known address is), or if it's a registered watercraft with a Department of Wildlife Resources title lien, the owner must also notify that lienholder of record by certified mail at least 10 days before the proposed auction Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

Step 3: The public auction

Chapter 29 does not require a newspaper ad or other public advertisement beyond the notices above Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. The auction must be a public auction for cash, and it may be held Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]:

  1. at the self-service storage facility
  2. at the nearest suitable place to where the property is held or stored, or
  3. online

A good-faith buyer takes the property free and clear of the rights of people against whom the lien was valid Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. So it matters to act before the sale date.

Cars: while a motor vehicle remains in the leased space, the owner also has a lien under the separate keeper-of-vehicles statute, Va. Code § 46.2-644.01, which expressly includes self-storage facilities Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3] Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[8]. That statute has its own notice and reclaim rules (including checking the title for existing liens and a Department of Motor Vehicles notice process) Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[8]. Unauthorized or trespassing vehicles or watercraft may be towed under local private-property towing ordinances Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3].

Boats: if you've been in default for more than 60 days, the owner may have a watercraft towed instead of selling it. It must send notice by verified mail or email to your last known address at least 10 days before the tow date, naming the tow company (this can be part of the further notice in Step 2). After the tow company takes the watercraft, the owner is immune from civil liability for damage that happens afterward Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

How to stop the sale

At any time before the public auction, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien and redeem the personal property Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the auction is cancelled.

Chapter 29 doesn't say a partial payment stops the auction, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, answer the notice using the contact details it must include Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4], in writing if you can. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

From the auction proceeds, the owner may satisfy its lien and must hold any balance for delivery on demand to you or to another lienholder referred to in the chapter. It does not have to keep holding a balance for a notified lienholder of record (or other lien creditor) that fails to claim an interest within 30 days of the public auction Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

So long as the owner complies with Chapter 29, its liability to you is limited to the net proceeds received from the auction of your personal property Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].

Chapter 29 does not set a specific number of years after which leftover money automatically goes to the state Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Ask the facility in writing for the sale results and any balance as soon as you learn of a sale, and make sure it has your current address. If money later sits unclaimed, you can search for it on vaMoneySearch.gov, the Virginia Department of the Treasury's official unclaimed property site Virginia Department of the Treasury[9] Virginia Department of the Treasury[10].

If the rental ends (and you still owe nothing for rent)

Separate from a money default, if personal property is left in the space after termination or nonrenewal of the rental agreement, the owner may treat it as abandoned. Before disposing of it, the owner must give you written notice of the termination or nonrenewal date and state that it will dispose of leftover property within 24 hours after a 10-day period from that notice. You may remove the property at any reasonable time (subject to reasonable restrictions) until it's disposed of Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[6].

That abandoned-property section does not apply when the default is a failure to meet a financial obligation; then §§ 55.1-2901 and 55.1-2902 (or other available remedies) apply instead Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[6].

If the owner broke the rules

Chapter 29 doesn't create a special statutory damages amount for noncompliance the way some other states do. The owner may also use other legal remedies the Act doesn't limit Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[5]. To complain about a facility, you can contact the Virginia Attorney General's consumer protection team Office of the Attorney General of Virginia[11].

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Virginia?
After default, the owner sends a first notice. If you don't cure within 10 days after the default, the owner may proceed. Before the sale, it must send a further verified-mail (or permitted electronic) notice that gives you at least 20 days after the date of that notice to pay. The auction can't go forward until that payment window runs out Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].
Can a Virginia storage facility lock me out?
Yes. Whenever you're in default under the rental agreement, the owner has the right to deny you access to the leased space Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Default means failing to perform any duty in the agreement or the Act on time Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[2].
Can a Virginia storage facility email the auction notice?
Only if your rental agreement expressly allows electronic notice. The first default notice may then go by electronic means; the further (pre-sale) notice may also go electronically if the sender keeps proof of delivery, such as an electronic receipt, a fax confirmation, or a certificate of service Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Otherwise, the further notice must go by verified mail Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].
Does Virginia require newspaper ads before a storage auction?
Chapter 29 does not require a newspaper or other public advertisement of the sale. The further notice to you must conspicuously state the time and place of the public auction Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. The auction may be held at the facility, at the nearest suitable place, or online Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].
What happens to leftover money after a Virginia storage auction?
The owner may take what it is owed from the sale proceeds and must hold any balance for delivery on demand to you (or to another lienholder covered by the chapter). A notified lienholder of record that doesn't claim an interest within 30 days of the auction need not have a balance held for it Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4]. Ask the facility in writing for the sale results and any balance right away.
What happens to my car or boat in a Virginia storage unit?
Motor vehicles stored in the unit are also subject to the separate "keeper of vehicles" lien in Va. Code § 46.2-644.01, which expressly includes self-storage facilities Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[8] Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[3]. For watercraft, if you've been in default more than 60 days, the owner may have the watercraft towed instead of selling it, after at least 10 days' verified-mail or email notice naming the tow company Virginia's Legislative Information System (law.lis.virginia.gov)[4].
