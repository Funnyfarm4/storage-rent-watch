New Jersey Storage Unit Auction Rules (N.J.S.A. 2A:44-187 to 2A:44-193): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

New Jersey storage auction rules: 30 days overdue, 14-day notice, two weekly ads, sale 15+ days later, late fee limit, redeeming your things, leftover money.

New Jersey's self-storage law is the Self-Service Storage Facility Act, N.J.S.A. 2A:44-187 to 2A:44-193 Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant, and the owner includes the operator, lessor or sublessor, the owner's agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor, or other reasonable charges due under the rental agreement, plus expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably incurred selling it. The lien attaches on the date your property is brought to the facility Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[3]. It ranks above other liens and security interests except a few listed by statute (including certain tax liens) and those you've told the owner about in writing Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[3].

If the owner issues a warehouse receipt, bill of lading or other document of title for your property, this Act doesn't apply; New Jersey's warehouse rules do instead Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[5].

Late fees

On top of its other remedies, the owner may charge a reasonable late fee for each month your rent is in default. The law says a late fee of up to $20 or 18% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is reasonable and isn't a penalty Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[4]. This was added in 2018 New Jersey Legislature[10].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: More than 30 days overdue

The owner can use the sale process only for a claim that is more than 30 days overdue Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].

Step 2: The notice (at least 14 days to pay)

You must be notified in person, or by verified mail or email to your last known address Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Verified mail means any USPS or private delivery service method that provides evidence of mailing. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep it current Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[2].

The notice must include Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]:

  • an itemized statement of the owner's claim, showing the amount due when the notice is sent and the date it became due
  • a brief, general description of the property, good enough for you to identify it (a locked or sealed container can be described without listing what's inside)
  • a notice of denial of access, if your rental agreement allows the owner to deny access, with the name, street address and phone number of the owner or agent you can contact
  • a demand for payment within a stated time not less than 14 days after delivery of the notice
  • a conspicuous statement that unless the claim is paid in time, your property will be advertised for sale, with the time and place of the sale

A mailed notice is presumed delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service or a private delivery service, properly addressed and prepaid. If the owner emails the notice and doesn't get an electronic receipt showing it reached your email address, the notice is presumed delivered only when it's sent to you by verified mail Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. These rules were updated in 2013 New Jersey Legislature[9].

Step 3: Two weekly ads

After the time in the notice runs out, the sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation where the facility is located. The ad must include a general description of the property, the facility address, the space number (if any) and your name, and the time, place and manner of the sale Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. If there's no such newspaper, the ad must instead be posted at least 10 days before the sale in at least 6 conspicuous places in the facility's neighborhood Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].

A 2025 New Jersey law changed where required legal notices are published: after March 1, 2026, a business or person required by law to publish a legal notice must publish it on an eligible online news publication, and those publications may not charge a fee or require registration or a subscription to view legal notices New Jersey Legislature[11]. If you're behind on rent, it's worth searching local online news sites' legal notice sections for your name or facility.

Step 4: The sale (15 days or more after the last ad)

The sale can't take place sooner than 15 days after the final publication of the ad, and it must follow the terms of the notice Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. It must be public and held at the facility or the nearest suitable place to where the property is stored Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. An online, publicly accessible auction website counts as a suitable place, as long as all the notice and other requirements are met Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[7].

A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights, even if the owner didn't follow the rules. The owner's liability from the sale is limited to the net proceeds Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].

Vehicles and boats: if rent and other charges for a motor vehicle or watercraft are in default for 60 consecutive days, the owner may have it towed. It must send you, by verified mail or email to your last known address, the towing company's name, address and phone number and the street address where you can redeem it. Once the tower takes it, the owner isn't liable for it Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].

How to stop the sale

Before the sale, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the owner incurred, and redeem your property. Once the owner receives the payment, it must return your property Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The Act doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner may pay its lien from the sale proceeds, but must deposit any balance in an interest-bearing account and give you notice of the amount, where it's deposited, and your right to get it Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. The Act doesn't set a deadline for claiming it, so ask the owner in writing for the balance as soon as you can.

If you can't reach the owner, you can also search New Jersey's official unclaimed property site for money held in your name State of New Jersey[12].

Other New Jersey rules

  • Who can sue: besides other remedies under the law, only an occupant listed on the last known rental agreement who is injured by a violation of the Act may bring a civil action for damages Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[8].
  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement limits the value of what you may store, that limit is the maximum value of your stored property, as long as the clause is printed in bold type or underlined Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[8].
  • Identity theft: the owner isn't liable for misuse of information in documents or storage media sold with your property if it didn't actually know about them Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Remove papers and drives with personal information if you can.
  • No living in units: no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[2].
  • Rent increases: the Act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in New Jersey?
The owner can begin only once its claim is more than 30 days overdue. It must then send a notice giving you at least 14 days after delivery to pay, advertise the sale once a week for two consecutive weeks after that deadline, and hold the sale no sooner than 15 days after the final ad Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].
Can a New Jersey storage facility send auction notices by email?
Yes. The notice may be delivered in person or sent by verified mail or email to your last known address. If the owner emails it and doesn't get an electronic receipt showing delivery, the notice is presumed delivered only when it is sent by verified mail to your last known address Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep it current Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[2].
Can a New Jersey storage facility lock me out?
The notice may include a notice of denial of access to your property if your rental agreement permits it, with the name, street address and phone number of the person to contact Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Check your rental agreement for its lockout terms.
How do I stop a storage auction in New Jersey?
Before the sale, pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the owner incurred. The owner must then return your property Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
Can New Jersey storage auctions be held online?
Yes. An online, publicly accessible auction website is a suitable place for the public sale, as long as the notice and other requirements of N.J.S.A. 2A:44-191 are met Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[7].
Can they tow my car or boat from a New Jersey storage unit?
If rent and other charges for a motor vehicle or watercraft are in default for 60 consecutive days, the owner may have it towed. It must send you, by verified mail or email, the towing company's name, address and phone number and the street address where you can redeem it Justia (full text of the New Jersey Statutes)[6].
