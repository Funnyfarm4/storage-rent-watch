Missouri Storage Unit Auction Rules (RSMo 415.400 to 415.425): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Missouri storage auction rules: sale only after 45+ days in default, two notices, 10+ days to pay, a newspaper ad 7 days before, late fees and leftover money.

Quick answer

  • You're in default when you fail to perform on time any obligation in your rental agreement, such as paying rent Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3].
  • Once you're in default, the operator may deny you access to your unit Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7].
  • The operator can sell only if you've been in default more than 45 days, and must send the first notice at least 45 days before the sale Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5].

Missouri's self-storage law is the Self-Service Storage Facilities Act, RSMo 415.400 to 415.425 Missouri Revisor of Statutes[2] Missouri Revisor of Statutes[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant (a person, lessee, sublessee, successor or assignee entitled to use the space), and the operator is the owner, operator, lessor or sublessor, or an agent or other person authorized to manage the facility Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text, cited by section; your rental agreement matters too.

The operator's lien

The operator has a lien on all personal property stored in your space for rent, labor or other charges, and for expenses reasonably incurred in selling the property. It has priority over all other liens except liens that were perfected and recorded on the property Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 1).

Your rental agreement must contain a statement in bold type telling you about the lien, that your property may be sold to satisfy it if you default, and that leftover sale money will go to the state treasurer if you don't claim it within one year after the sale Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 1).

Before you move anything in, you must give the operator (or list in the agreement) the name and address of anyone with a valid lien on your property and of any third-party owner of what you store, with a description of that property. The operator must also tell you in writing whether or not it has casualty insurance on your property. If the agreement limits the value of what you may store, that limit is treated as the maximum value of your property and the most the owner owes for loss or damage Missouri Revisor of Statutes[4].

You're in default when you fail to perform on time any obligation or duty in the rental agreement Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3]. Until a lien sale, and unless your agreement says otherwise, you keep exclusive care, custody and control of what you store Missouri Revisor of Statutes[8].

Late fees

A late fee must be stated in your rental agreement (or an addendum); otherwise it can't be collected. The operator may charge a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent when due. A late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, for each late payment is deemed reasonable and not a penalty. The operator can set a higher fee only if it's reasonable, and it has the burden of proving that Missouri Revisor of Statutes[6].

On top of late fees, the operator may recover all reasonable rent collection and lien enforcement expenses from you Missouri Revisor of Statutes[6] (subsection 6). Ask for an itemized list of any such charges.

Lockouts

If you're in default, the operator may deny you access to your leased space Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7] (subsection 3). The operator may also enter the space whenever reasonably necessary to protect and preserve the facility or the property stored there Missouri Revisor of Statutes[4] (subsection 2).

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Default

Default starts as soon as you miss an obligation in your agreement, such as rent, on time Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3]. The operator may lock you out from then on Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7].

Step 2: First notice (at least 45 days before the sale)

At least 45 days before any sale, the operator must notify you of the default by first-class mail or email at your last known address. It must also notify each lienholder you listed and any third-party owner you identified. This 45-day notice period runs at the same time as the 45-day default period, not after it Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 4).

Step 3: Second notice (at least 10 days to pay)

No sooner than 10 days after mailing the first notice, the operator must send a second notice by verified mail (any Postal Service or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing) or email Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3] Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5]. It must include Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 4):

  • a statement that the contents of your space are subject to the operator's lien
  • the operator's claim: the charges due on the date of the notice, any additional charges that will come due before the date of release for sale, and when they come due
  • a demand for payment within a set time, not less than 10 days after the second notice was mailed
  • a statement that unless you pay in time, the contents will be sold after a specified time
  • the name, street address and phone number of the operator or a designated agent you can contact

How notices count: a mailed notice is deemed delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service, properly addressed and stamped; an emailed notice counts when it's sent to your last known email address Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7] (subsection 4). Your last known address is the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or in a later written change-of-address notice, and you can designate one of them in writing as your preferred contact method, which the operator must use Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3]. Keep both current with the facility in writing.

Step 4: Advertising the sale (at least 7 days before)

At least 7 days before the sale, the operator must advertise the time, place and terms of the sale in the classified section of a newspaper of general circulation where the sale will be held. The ad must say the items will be released for sale Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 4).

Step 5: The sale (after more than 45 days in default)

The operator can sell only if you've been in default for more than 45 days. The sale must be for cash and may be public or private, as one unit or in parcels, at any time or place and on any terms, as long as it's done in a commercially reasonable manner Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 2). A public sale can be held at the facility or online on a publicly accessible website Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3]. Under the commercial-reasonableness rules the act points to, a sale is generally reasonable if it's made in the usual way on a recognized market, at the current market price, or in line with reasonable practices among dealers in that kind of property, and the fact that a higher price was possible doesn't by itself make it unreasonable Missouri Revisor of Statutes[9].

The operator may otherwise dispose of property that has no commercial value, which includes property offered for sale in a commercially reasonable way that gets no bid or offer Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3].

Vehicles, boats and trailers: if the property is a vehicle, watercraft or trailer and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days, the owner may treat it as an abandoned vehicle and have it towed. Once the tow company takes possession, the facility owner isn't liable for any damage to it Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 5).

How to stop the sale

At any time before the sale, you may pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien and redeem your property Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 6). Ask for the exact payoff amount in writing (rent, late fees and any collection or enforcement expenses), pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The law doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

A buyer in good faith takes your property free and clear of your rights and other liens, so act before the sale Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7] (subsection 1).

Leftover money from the sale

Sale money first pays the lien. Any surplus is held for 1 year after the sale for delivery on demand to you or to other lienholders the operator knows about or you listed Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 3).

To be paid, you must give the operator a sworn affidavit stating that there are no other valid liens on the property sold and that you'll indemnify the operator for damages or money it pays because of claims from other lienholders Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 3). Ask the facility in writing for your balance as soon as you can, and keep a copy.

After the 1-year period, the leftover money is treated as abandoned property and is reported and paid to the Missouri State Treasurer under the unclaimed property law Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5] (subsection 3). The Treasurer's Office lets you search for unclaimed property and track a claim online, and says it never charges to return unclaimed property Missouri State Treasurer[10].

If the operator breaks the rules

  • Liability limit depends on following the law: the operator's liability to you is limited to the net proceeds of the sale only if it complied with the act Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7] (subsection 2). If it didn't follow the notice and sale rules, talk to a lawyer about your options.
  • Complain to the state: you can file a consumer complaint with the Missouri Attorney General's Office, which enforces the Merchandising Practices Act and mediates complaints between consumers and businesses Missouri Attorney General's Office[11].
  • The act itself doesn't spell out a separate penalty or a specific lawsuit right for violations Missouri Revisor of Statutes[1], which is another reason to get free legal help early.

Other Missouri rules

  • No living in units: an occupant may not use a leased space for residential purposes, and the operator may not knowingly allow it Missouri Revisor of Statutes[4] (subsection 1).
  • Rent increases: the act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Missouri Revisor of Statutes[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Missouri?
The law sets minimums, not a fixed date. The operator can sell only after you've been in default for more than 45 days. It must send a first notice at least 45 days before the sale, a second notice no sooner than 10 days after the first that gives you at least 10 days to pay, and a newspaper ad at least 7 days before the sale Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5].
Can a Missouri storage facility send auction notices by email?
Yes. The law lets the first notice go by first-class mail or email, and the second notice by verified mail or email, to your last known address. That can be the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or in a later written change-of-address notice, and you can designate one of them in writing as your preferred contact method, which the operator must then use Missouri Revisor of Statutes[3] Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5].
Can a Missouri storage facility lock me out?
Yes. If you're in default, the operator may deny you access to your leased space Missouri Revisor of Statutes[7].
What is a legal late fee for a storage unit in Missouri?
A late fee must be written in your rental agreement or an addendum, or it can't be collected. The operator may charge a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent when due, and $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, for each late payment is deemed reasonable. A higher fee is allowed only if it's reasonable, and the operator has the burden of proving that. The operator may also recover reasonable rent collection and lien enforcement expenses on top of late fees Missouri Revisor of Statutes[6].
Can they tow my car from a Missouri storage unit?
Yes. If the property is a vehicle, watercraft or trailer and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days, the owner may treat it as abandoned and have it towed. Once the tow company takes possession, the facility owner isn't liable for damage to it Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5].
What happens to the leftover money after a Missouri storage auction?
Sale money first pays the lien. Any surplus is held for 1 year for delivery on demand to you or to other lienholders the operator knows about. To get it, you must give the operator a sworn affidavit that there are no other valid liens on the property and that you'll indemnify the operator against claims from other lienholders. After 1 year, unclaimed money goes to the Missouri State Treasurer as unclaimed property Missouri Revisor of Statutes[5], where you can search and file a claim Missouri State Treasurer[10].
