Georgia Storage Unit Auction Rules (O.C.G.A. 10-4-210): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

How a storage unit gets sold in Georgia: 30 days of default, a 14-day notice, one ad, a 15-day wait, the late-fee cap and the 2-year rule for leftover money.

Quick answer

  • You're in default if a monthly payment isn't made by the seventh calendar day of the month it's due, and the owner can deny you access once you're in default Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
  • The owner can start enforcing its lien only after you've been in default continuously for 30 days Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
  • The lien notice must demand payment within a time not less than 14 days after the notice is delivered Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
  • The sale is advertised once in the county legal organ (or another commercially reasonable way for non-vehicles), and can't happen sooner than 15 days after publication Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
  • Late fees are capped at $20 or 20% of monthly rent, whichever is greater, for each month a payment is late Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[8].
  • Leftover sale money is held for you; if it's not claimed within two years, it goes through Georgia's unclaimed property process Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5] Georgia Department of Revenue[10].

Georgia's self-storage law is the Georgia Self-service Storage Facility Act of 2013, O.C.G.A. §§ 10-4-210 to 10-4-218 Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[2] Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[1]. The sale process is in § 10-4-213, which spells it out as required wording for your rental agreement Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. In Georgia law the renter is called the occupant Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[3]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement can add terms of its own Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[6], so read it too.

The owner's lien

The facility has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, late fees, labor or other charges related to the property, plus expenses needed to preserve it and expenses reasonably incurred selling or disposing of it. The lien attaches the day your property is brought to the facility Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[4].

"Personal property" includes vehicles, trailers, watercraft, household items and furnishings Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[3].

When you're in default

Under the required agreement wording, you're in default if a monthly payment isn't made by the seventh calendar day of the month it's due, or if your check bounces. Once you're in default, the owner may deny you access to your unit, without notice, until it gets paid Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

The owner can start the lien sale process only after you've been in default continuously for 30 days Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

If you never signed a written agreement the owner delivered by hand or verified mail, continuing to use the unit for 14 days can count as accepting it, but only if the agreement carries a specific bold-type warning saying so Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[9].

Late fees

If your rental agreement allows it, the owner can charge a late fee of no more than $20 per month or 20% of the monthly rent per month, whichever is greater, for each month a payment is late. This cap applies to agreements entered into, extended or renewed, and late payments, on or after July 1, 2019 Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[8].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: The lien notice

The notice must be in writing and delivered in person, by verified mail or by email Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. "Verified mail" means certified, registered or overnight delivery, or another method where the post office or delivery service gives proof the item was sent Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[3]. The owner must also notify anyone with a superior lien on the property, such as a lender you disclosed Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

If the owner emails the notice and gets neither a non-automated reply nor a delivery receipt, it must send the notice by verified mail to your last known address (or your designated agent's) before going ahead with the sale Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. Your last known address is the street, PO box or email address in your latest rental agreement, or one you later gave in a written change-of-address notice Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[3], so keep it current.

The notice must Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]:

  • include an itemized statement of the claim, showing the amount due and the date it became due
  • tell you that you've been denied access to your property
  • give the name, street address, email address and phone number of the owner or its agent you can contact
  • demand payment within a set time not less than 14 days after delivery
  • state that unless you pay in time, the property will be advertised and sold at a public sale to the highest bidder at a specified time and place

Step 2: Advertising the sale

After the notice deadline passes, the owner must advertise the sale once in the legal organ for the county where the facility is located. For anything other than a motor vehicle, motorcycle, trailer, watercraft or recreational vehicle, the ad may instead be done in any other commercially reasonable way Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

The ad must include a brief general description of the property, the facility's address, the unit number if any, your name, and the time, place and manner of the sale Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

What changed in 2025: Georgia used to require ads once a week for two consecutive weeks, with the sale at least 15 days after the first ad. A 2025 law cut this to a single ad, with the sale at least 15 days after publication, for lien enforcement started on or after July 1, 2025 Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. Older guides online may still describe the two-week rule.

Step 3: The sale

The sale can't take place sooner than 15 days after publication Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. It must be held at the facility or the nearest suitable place, and a public sale includes offering the property on a publicly accessible website that regularly runs online auctions Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. If at least 3 independent bidders attend, the ad and the sale are deemed commercially reasonable Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

If no one buys the property, and the owner followed the rules, it may dispose of the property another way, and it must tell you what it did Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. A good-faith buyer takes the property even if the owner didn't follow every rule Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5], which is why it matters to act before the sale date.

Vehicles, trailers and boats: if rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days after they were due, the owner may have the vehicle, trailer or watercraft towed instead of selling it, and isn't liable for it once the tow company takes it Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

Military renters: if the rental agreement is with a service member, the owner must follow the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[6].

How to stop the sale

Before any sale or other disposition, you can pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses incurred, and redeem your property Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. Pay in a way that gives you a receipt, and ask for written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.

If you think the amount is wrong, compare it with the itemized statement in your notice and the late-fee cap above Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5] Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[8]. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner can pay itself what it's owed from the sale proceeds, but must hold any balance for you (or a secured lender it notified). If the balance isn't claimed within two years of the sale, it's handled under Georgia's Disposition of Unclaimed Property Act Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5]. The Georgia Department of Revenue runs the state's unclaimed property program Georgia Department of Revenue[10], and you can search for money in your name on its official Georgia Unclaimed Property Search site Georgia Department of Revenue[11].

Don't wait two years: ask the facility in writing for the sale results and your balance as soon as you learn your unit was sold. Under the required agreement wording, the owner's liability can't exceed the sale proceeds, and if your agreement limits the value you may store, that limit is treated as the maximum value of your property Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].

To complain about a facility, you can contact the Georgia Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division Georgia Attorney General[12].

If the facility ends your rental

The owner can end or not renew your rental agreement by written notice delivered by hand or verified mail. The notice must give you at least 14 days after delivery to remove everything. Until then the owner can limit access to removing your things during normal business hours, and it may dispose of anything left after the date in the notice Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[9].

Other Georgia rules

  • No living in units: no occupant may use a storage facility for residential purposes Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[7].
  • Rent increases: the Act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[1] Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[6]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Georgia?
The owner can start enforcing its lien only after 30 days of continuous default. The notice must then give you at least 14 days after delivery to pay, the sale is advertised once, and the sale can't be held sooner than 15 days after publication Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
Can a Georgia storage facility send the lien notice by email?
Yes. The notice can be delivered in person, by verified mail or by email. If the owner emails it and gets neither a non-automated reply nor a delivery receipt, it must send the notice by verified mail to your last known address before going ahead with the sale Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
What is the maximum late fee for a storage unit in Georgia?
If your rental agreement allows a late fee, it can be no more than $20 per month or 20% of the monthly rent per month, whichever is greater. That cap applies to agreements entered into, extended or renewed, and late payments, on or after July 1, 2019 Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[8].
Do Georgia storage auctions still need two weeks of newspaper ads?
No. For lien enforcement started on or after July 1, 2025, the sale is advertised once in the legal organ for the county, or in another commercially reasonable way when the property isn't a vehicle, trailer, watercraft or RV. The sale can't be sooner than 15 days after publication Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[5].
Can I live in my storage unit in Georgia?
No. Georgia law says no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Justia (Official Code of Georgia text)[7].
