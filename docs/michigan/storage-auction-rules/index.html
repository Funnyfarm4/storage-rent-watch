Michigan Storage Unit Auction Rules (MCL 570.521): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

How Michigan storage units get sold: lockout after 5 days, a 14-day payment notice, 2 weeks of ads, a $20 or 20% late-fee rule, and 2 years to claim leftovers.

Quick answer

  • If rent isn't paid, the owner may lock you out without notice starting 5 days after the rent was due Michigan Legislature[4].
  • Before any sale, you must get a written notice (in person, first-class mail or email) demanding payment within at least 14 days after delivery Michigan Legislature[5].
  • After that deadline, the sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks, and the sale can't happen sooner than 15 days after the first ad Michigan Legislature[5].
  • A monthly late fee of $20 or 20% of monthly rent, whichever is greater, is treated as reasonable; the owner must justify anything higher Michigan Legislature[3].
  • You can stop the sale by paying the lien plus reasonable enforcement expenses before the sale; the owner must then return your things Michigan Legislature[5].
  • Leftover sale money is mailed to you by certified mail; if you don't claim it within 2 years, it goes to the State of Michigan Michigan Legislature[5].

Michigan's self-storage law is the Self-Service Storage Facility Act, 1985 PA 148, found at MCL 570.521 to 570.527 Michigan Legislature[1]. The sale process is in section 570.525 Michigan Legislature[5]. In Michigan law the renter is called the tenant, and "owner" includes the operator, manager or anyone authorized to collect rent Michigan Legislature[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement can create other liens by contract Michigan Legislature[6], so read it too.

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility, whether or not you own it, for rent and other lawful storage charges, including expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably spent selling it under the Act. The lien attaches when your property arrives or when you sign the rental agreement, whichever comes first Michigan Legislature[3].

When you sign up, the owner must give you a written notice explaining that you'll be notified by first-class mail or email before any sale, that you should report address changes in writing, and that you should name another person who can reach you; that person gets notified the same way you do Michigan Legislature[3].

Your rental agreement may set a limit on the value of what you store, and that limit counts as the maximum value of your property for all purposes Michigan Legislature[3]. If someone other than you will use the unit, you must disclose their name, address and phone number in writing Michigan Legislature[4].

The Act doesn't apply if the owner issued you a warehouse receipt, bill of lading or other document of title Michigan Legislature[7].

Late fees and lockouts

Your agreement may charge a reasonable late fee. A monthly late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is considered reasonable and not a penalty, and the owner has the burden of justifying any higher late fee Michigan Legislature[3].

If you don't pay rent when it's due, the owner may deny you access without notice, but not until at least 5 days after the due date Michigan Legislature[4].

The Act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement Michigan Legislature[1].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: The written notice and payment demand

The owner can enforce its lien only the way section 570.525 allows Michigan Legislature[5]. First, you (and any occupant you named) must get a written notice of the owner's intent to enforce the lien, delivered in person, by first-class mail or by email to your last known address Michigan Legislature[5]. Your last known address is the street address, P.O. box or email address in your latest rental agreement, or in a later written change-of-address notice you hand-delivered, mailed or emailed Michigan Legislature[2].

The notice must include Michigan Legislature[5]:

  • an itemized statement of the amount due when the notice was sent and the date it became due
  • a demand for payment within a set time not less than 14 days after delivery
  • a conspicuous statement that, unless you pay in time, your property will be advertised and sold or otherwise disposed of at a set time and place
  • the owner's name, street address and phone number so you can respond
  • a statement about service members' rights if deployed overseas for 180 days or more

A mailed notice is presumed delivered once it's properly addressed, stamped and deposited with the Postal Service, and an email is presumed delivered when it's sent to your last known email address Michigan Legislature[5]. So the clock can start even if you never read it.

After the deadline in the notice passes, the owner may move your things to another storage space while the sale is pending Michigan Legislature[5].

Step 2: Advertising the sale

After the notice deadline, the sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks, either in the print or online version of a newspaper of general circulation in the area, or on a publicly available website named in your rental agreement. If there's no such newspaper and no website named in your agreement, the ad must be posted in at least 3 conspicuous places in the neighborhood at least 10 days before the sale Michigan Legislature[5].

The ad must include a brief, general inventory of the property, the facility's address, your name, and the time, place and manner of the sale. A locked, sealed or tied container can be listed as just that Michigan Legislature[5].

Step 3: The sale

The sale can't take place sooner than 15 days after the first ad Michigan Legislature[5]. It must match what the notices said and be conducted in a commercially reasonable manner Michigan Legislature[5].

A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights even if the owner didn't follow the rules Michigan Legislature[5], which is why it matters to act before the sale date. Your remedy at that point is a damages claim (see below).

The owner may destroy documents it reasonably believes contain confidential or personal identification information instead of selling them, and may dispose of property it can't lawfully sell Michigan Legislature[5].

Cars, boats and trailers: if rent stays unpaid for 60 days, the owner may have a motor vehicle, aircraft, mobile home, moped, motorcycle, snowmobile, trailer or watercraft towed by a licensed carrier instead of selling it, and isn't responsible for it once the carrier takes it. Before selling one of these, the owner must check with the Secretary of State for title holders and lienholders and notify them of the sale Michigan Legislature[5].

Service members: if you're transferred or deployed overseas on active duty for 180 days or more, you can notify the owner in writing with evidence. The owner then can't enforce its lien until 90 days after your overseas service ends Michigan Legislature[4] Michigan Legislature[5].

How to stop the sale

Before the sale, you can pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the owner incurred enforcing it, and redeem your property. Once the owner receives that amount, it must return your property Michigan Legislature[5].

If you don't redeem or satisfy the lien, the law treats your property as abandoned and the owner may take back the unit Michigan Legislature[5]. The Act doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing.

If the amount looks wrong, answer the notice using the contact details it must include Michigan Legislature[5], in writing if you can. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

Sale money is paid out in this order Michigan Legislature[5]:

  1. The owner's liens, up to the equivalent of 4 months' rent
  2. Any earlier perfected lienholders (such as a lender with a security interest)
  3. The rest of the owner's liens, including unpaid rent, late fees and reasonable enforcement expenses

Anything left over must be mailed to you by certified mail at your last known address, with a separate first-class mail notice. If you don't claim it within 2 years after the sale, it escheats (goes) to the State of Michigan, and the owner's sale records are subject to audit by the Department of Treasury Michigan Legislature[5].

So if your unit was sold, ask the facility in writing for the sale results and any balance, and make sure it has your current address. After the money goes to the state, you can search for it on Michigan Unclaimed Property, the Treasury's official site Michigan Department of Treasury[8].

If the owner broke the rules

If you suffer damages because the owner didn't comply with the Act, you can sue for your actual damages or $250, whichever is greater, plus reasonable attorney fees Michigan Legislature[6]. To complain about a facility, you can also contact the Michigan Attorney General's consumer protection team Michigan Department of Attorney General[9].

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Michigan?
The owner must first send a notice giving you at least 14 days after delivery to pay. After that deadline passes, the sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks, and it can't take place sooner than 15 days after the first ad Michigan Legislature[5].
Can a Michigan storage facility lock me out?
Yes. If you don't pay rent when it's due, the owner may deny you access to your property without notice, but not until at least 5 days after the rent was due Michigan Legislature[4].
Can a Michigan storage facility send the lien notice by email?
Yes. The notice may be delivered in person, by first-class mail or by email to your last known address, and an email notice is presumed delivered when it's sent to your last known email address Michigan Legislature[5]. Your last known address is the one in your latest rental agreement or in a later written change-of-address notice Michigan Legislature[2], so keep it current.
What is the maximum storage late fee in Michigan?
The law doesn't set a hard cap. It says a monthly late fee of $20 or 20% of monthly rent, whichever is greater, is reasonable and not a penalty, and the owner has the burden of justifying any higher late fee Michigan Legislature[3].
What happens to my car or boat in a Michigan storage unit?
If rent stays unpaid for 60 days, the owner may have a motor vehicle, aircraft, mobile home, moped, motorcycle, snowmobile, trailer or watercraft towed instead of selling it. Before selling one, the owner must check with the Secretary of State for title holders and lienholders and notify them of the sale Michigan Legislature[5].
I'm in the military and deployed. Can my unit be sold?
If you're a service member transferred or deployed overseas on active duty for 180 days or more, you can notify the owner in writing with evidence of the deployment. The owner then can't enforce its lien until 90 days after your overseas service ends Michigan Legislature[4] Michigan Legislature[5].
