Indiana Storage Unit Auction Rules (IC 26-3-8): Timeline, Notices and Your Rights

How a storage unit gets sold in Indiana: the 5-day, 30-day and 60-day deadlines, required notices and ads, late fee limits, and leftover sale money.

Last checked against official sources: Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026

This is general legal information, not legal advice. We are not lawyers. Check the linked law and talk to a lawyer or free legal aid about your situation.

Quick answer

  • A facility can start enforcing its lien after you've been in default continuously for at least 5 days Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].
  • It must send a written notice by email or verified mail giving you at least 30 days from mailing to pay Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].
  • The sale or other disposal can't be set for earlier than 60 days after your default Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].
  • The sale must be advertised, and held at least 10 days after the ad Justia (Indiana Code text)[10].
  • You can stop the sale by paying enough to satisfy the lien before the sale; the owner must then return your property immediately Justia (Indiana Code text)[9].
  • Money left over after the sale is held for you on demand. After 1 year it goes to Indiana's unclaimed property program Justia (Indiana Code text)[11].

Indiana's self-storage law is Indiana Code 26-3-8, "Self-Service Storage Facilities" Justia (Indiana Code text)[1]. The official text is published by the Indiana General Assembly Indiana General Assembly[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of that chapter as published in the 2025 Indiana Code. Your rental agreement can add terms of its own, so read it too.

What counts as default

"Default" means not doing, on time, something the rental agreement requires, such as paying rent Justia (Indiana Code text)[3]. The facility's lien attaches the day you put property in the unit Justia (Indiana Code text)[6], and every Indiana storage rental agreement must include a bold statement telling you about the lien and how the owner can enforce it Justia (Indiana Code text)[6].

What the lien covers

The owner's lien can cover Justia (Indiana Code text)[6]:

  • rent, labor and other charges under the rental agreement, including late fees and rent collection costs
  • expenses needed to preserve your property
  • reasonable expenses of the sale or other disposal, including lien enforcement costs

Late fee cap: for each month you're in default, the late fee can't be more than the greater of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent. The owner can also recover "reasonable" costs of collecting rent and enforcing the lien Justia (Indiana Code text)[7].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Day 5 of continuous default

The owner may begin enforcing the lien. It can deny you access to the facility and your unit, and move your things to another space Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].

Step 2: The written notice

The owner must send you a notice by email or "verified mail" (any USPS or private delivery method that gives evidence of mailing) Justia (Indiana Code text)[5], to your last known postal or email address Justia (Indiana Code text)[4]. The notice must include Justia (Indiana Code text)[8]:

  • an itemized statement of what you owe and when it became due
  • a demand to pay by a date at least 30 days after the notice was mailed
  • a statement that your unit's contents are subject to the lien, and whether you've been locked out or your property has been moved
  • a name, street address and phone number to contact
  • a conspicuous warning that unless you pay, your property will be advertised and sold or otherwise disposed of at a stated place and time at least 60 days after your default

Step 3: Advertising

After the payment deadline passes, the owner must advertise the sale once in a newspaper of general circulation in the county, or in another commercially reasonable way likely to attract at least three independent bidders. The ad must say the property will be sold to satisfy the lien, and give the facility address, unit number (if any), your name, and the manner, time and place of sale Justia (Indiana Code text)[10].

Step 4: The sale

It must happen at least 10 days after the newspaper ad or the first alternative ad Justia (Indiana Code text)[10], at the facility, the nearest suitable place, or on a publicly accessible website Justia (Indiana Code text)[11]. It must be held the way, and at the place and time, stated in your notice Justia (Indiana Code text)[8]. The owner may bid and buy Justia (Indiana Code text)[11].

Step 5: If it doesn't sell

The owner must tell you in writing, at your last known address, what else it plans to do with the property Justia (Indiana Code text)[10].

Vehicles, trailers and boats: instead of a sale, the owner may have them towed or removed from the facility if you don't pay by the notice deadline Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].

How to stop the sale

Before any sale or other disposal, you can redeem your property by paying the owner an amount sufficient to satisfy the lien. Once you pay, the owner must return your property immediately Justia (Indiana Code text)[9]. See our step-by-step guide: How to stop a storage auction in Indiana.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner can pay itself the lien amount from the sale. If the sale brings in more, the owner must hold the balance for you and deliver it when you ask. If you don't claim it within one year after the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property under IC 32-34-1.5 Justia (Indiana Code text)[11], which you can search for through the Indiana Attorney General's unclaimed property site Indiana Attorney General[13].

Value limits in your lease

A rental agreement may cap the value of what you can store. If it does, that cap is treated as the maximum value of your property in the unit Justia (Indiana Code text)[12]. Check this before you store anything valuable.

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Indiana?
The sale can't be set for earlier than 60 days after you default, and you must get at least 30 days from the mailing of the lien notice to pay Justia (Indiana Code text)[8]. The sale must also be at least 10 days after it's advertised Justia (Indiana Code text)[10].
Can an Indiana storage facility lock me out?
Yes. Once you've been in default continuously for at least 5 days, the owner enforcing its lien may deny you access to the facility and your unit Justia (Indiana Code text)[8].
Can they send the lien notice by email?
Yes. Indiana allows the notice by email or by verified mail to your last known address Justia (Indiana Code text)[8], which can be the postal or email address you gave in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice Justia (Indiana Code text)[4].
What is the maximum late fee for a storage unit in Indiana?
For each month in default, the greater of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, plus reasonable costs of rent collection and lien enforcement Justia (Indiana Code text)[7].
Can the storage facility owner buy my things at the auction?
Yes. Indiana law lets the owner buy at the sale Justia (Indiana Code text)[11].
