How to Stop a Storage Unit Auction in Indiana (and Claim Leftover Sale Money)

Steps to stop an Indiana storage lien sale before it happens, check the late fees and notice, and claim any money left over after a sale.

Quick answer

  • You have a legal right to redeem your property by paying enough to satisfy the owner's lien before the sale or other disposal Justia (Indiana Code text)[2].
  • When you pay, the owner must return your property immediately Justia (Indiana Code text)[2].
  • The sale can't be earlier than 60 days after default, and the notice must give you at least 30 days to pay Justia (Indiana Code text)[1].
  • Leftover sale money is held for you on demand; after 1 year it becomes unclaimed property Justia (Indiana Code text)[7].

If your Indiana storage unit is behind on rent and you've received a lien notice, you can still stop the sale. Indiana's storage law, IC 26-3-8 Justia (Indiana Code text)[10], gives you a right to redeem your property by paying before the sale Justia (Indiana Code text)[2]. Here's how to use it.

Step 1: Find the key dates on your notice

Your lien notice must list what you owe, the date it became due, a payment deadline at least 30 days after the notice was mailed, a contact name, street address and phone number, and the place and time of the planned sale, which must be at least 60 days after your default Justia (Indiana Code text)[1]. Write down the payment deadline and the sale date. The sale itself must be held at least 10 days after it's advertised Justia (Indiana Code text)[6].

Step 2: Check the amount

Ask for the itemized statement in writing if you don't have it. The lien can include rent, late fees, rent collection costs, preservation expenses and reasonable sale or lien enforcement expenses Justia (Indiana Code text)[3]. Check the late fees: for each month in default, Indiana caps the late fee at the greater of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent Justia (Indiana Code text)[4].

Step 3: Pay before the sale and get a receipt

Before any sale or other disposal, you can redeem your property by paying the owner an amount sufficient to satisfy the lien. The owner must then return your property immediately Justia (Indiana Code text)[2]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, get a written receipt that says the lien is paid in full, and ask the facility to confirm in writing that the sale is cancelled.

Step 4: If you can't pay it all

Indiana's redemption right is for the full lien amount Justia (Indiana Code text)[2]. Some facilities may agree to a payment plan or a short delay, but that's up to them. If you reach a deal, get it in writing, with the new deadline. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) the same day.

Step 5: Make sure your address is up to date

Notices go to your last known address, meaning the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or in a written change-of-address notice you gave the facility Justia (Indiana Code text)[5]. If you've moved or changed email, tell the facility in writing.

After a sale: claim the leftover money

If your unit was already sold, the facility can keep what it's owed from the sale. Any extra must be held for you and delivered when you ask. If you don't claim it within one year after the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property under Indiana law Justia (Indiana Code text)[7]. Search for it on Indiana's official unclaimed property site, run by the Attorney General Indiana Attorney General[8].

To complain about a storage facility, you can contact the Indiana Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division Indiana Attorney General[9].

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a partial payment to stop a storage auction in Indiana?
The redemption right in Indiana law is for an amount sufficient to satisfy the owner's lien Justia (Indiana Code text)[2]. Whether the facility accepts less, or a payment plan, is up to the facility, so get any deal in writing.
What if I never got the lien notice?
The notice has to be sent by email or verified mail to your last known address Justia (Indiana Code text)[1], which is the address you gave in your latest rental agreement or a later written change of address Justia (Indiana Code text)[5]. If you think the facility didn't follow the rules, talk to a legal aid office or lawyer right away.
How do I get the money left over after my unit was sold?
Ask the facility in writing for the balance; it must hold any surplus for you on demand. If you don't claim it within one year of the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property Justia (Indiana Code text)[7], which you can search for on Indiana's official unclaimed property site Indiana Attorney General[8].
Who can I complain to about a storage facility in Indiana?
You can contact the Indiana Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division Indiana Attorney General[9]. For your legal rights in a specific case, contact legal aid.
