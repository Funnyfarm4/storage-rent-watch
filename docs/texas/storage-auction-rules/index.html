Texas Storage Unit Auction Rules (Property Code Ch. 59): Timeline, Notices and Your Rights

How a Texas storage unit gets sold under Property Code chapter 59: the 14-day notice, two weeks of ads, the 15-day wait, redemption and leftover money.

Quick answer

  • A facility can seize and sell your things without a court judgment only if the contractual landlord's lien is underlined or in conspicuous bold print in a written rental agreement Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[11].
  • You get written notice of the claim and 14 days after it's delivered to pay Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[12] Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13].

Texas's self-storage lien law is Chapter 59 of the Texas Property Code, "Self-Service Storage Facility Liens" Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[1]. In it, the renter is called the tenant and the facility owner or manager is the lessor Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[2]. Below is a plain-English summary of the statute text. Your rental agreement matters too, but it can't waive the rights this chapter gives you unless the chapter itself allows it Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[3].

The facility's lien

The facility has a lien on all property in your unit for charges that are due and unpaid Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[10]. The lien attaches the day you put your property in the unit and takes priority over other liens on the same property Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[5].

Court order or contract lien

A facility can normally enforce its lien only with a court judgment that forecloses the lien and orders a sale. It can seize and sell your things without going to court only if Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[11]:

  • the seizure and sale are under a contractual landlord's lien that is underlined or printed in conspicuous bold in a written rental agreement, and
  • the seizure and sale follow chapter 59.

Check your agreement for that bold or underlined lien clause.

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Written notice of the claim

The facility must give you written notice of its claim Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[12]. The notice must contain Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13]:

  • an itemized account of the claim
  • the name, address and phone number of the lessor or its agent
  • a statement that the contents of your unit have been seized under the contractual landlord's lien
  • a statement that if you don't pay on or before the 14th day after the notice is delivered, the property may be sold at public auction (or, for vehicles and boats, towed to a vehicle storage facility)
  • an underlined or bold statement asking a tenant in military service to tell the facility about that service right away

The notice must be delivered in person, or by email or verified mail to the email or postal address in your rental agreement or in a later written notice you gave the facility. Verified mail means any mailing method that provides evidence of mailing Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[2]. Mailed notice counts as delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service or a carrier; email counts as delivered when sent Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13]. Email is allowed only if your written agreement says, underlined or in bold, that notice may be given by email if you choose to provide an email address Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13]. Keep your contact information current with the facility.

Step 2: Advertising the sale

If you don't pay by the 14th day after delivery, the facility must advertise the sale Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[12]. The notice must be published once in each of two consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation in the county where the facility is. Only if there's no such newspaper may the facility instead post the notice at the facility and at least five other conspicuous places nearby Justia (copy of Texas Property Code)[14]. The ad must include Justia (copy of Texas Property Code)[14]:

  • a general description of the property
  • a statement that it is being sold to satisfy a landlord's lien
  • your name
  • the facility's address
  • the time, place and terms of the sale (for an online sale, the place is the website address)

Step 3: The sale

With newspaper notice, the facility may not sell until the 15th day after the first publication. With posted notice, it may sell after the 10th day after posting Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[12]. The sale must be public, held at the facility, at a reasonably near public place, or on a website open to the public. It must follow the terms in the ad, and the property goes to the highest bidder Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[16]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your claim even if the facility didn't follow chapter 59 Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[6], so act before the sale.

Vehicles and boats: the facility must send a separate notice by verified mail to the last known owner and each recorded lienholder within 30 days of taking possession, and can't sell before the 31st day after that notice is mailed or published Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[15]. If your rental agreement allows it, the facility may instead have a titled vehicle or boat towed to a vehicle storage facility after the 14-day notice period Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[18].

How to stop the sale

You can redeem your property before it's sold or otherwise disposed of by paying the facility the amount of the lien plus its reasonable expenses under chapter 59 Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[7]. Ask for an itemized payoff amount in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help right away.

If you're a member of the Texas State Guard or Texas National Guard in military service, you get the same storage-lien protections as servicemembers under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[9]. Tell the facility in writing; it may ask for documentation Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13].

Leftover money from the sale

If the sale brings in more than the lien and the reasonable expenses of the sale, the facility must send written notice of the excess to your last known address. It holds the excess and must give it to you if you ask within two years after the sale. After two years, the facility owns it Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[17]. Unlike some states, Texas's storage chapter doesn't send this money to a state unclaimed property office, so ask the facility directly and in writing.

If the facility broke the rules

A person injured by a violation of chapter 59 may sue for damages under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[4]. You can also file a consumer complaint with the Texas Attorney General Office of the Texas Attorney General[19].

Other Texas rules to know

  • No living in the unit: a tenant may not use a storage unit as a residence or let anyone else do so Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[8].
  • Late fees and rent increases: chapter 59 has no section setting a late-fee cap or a rent-increase notice period, so your rental agreement controls those terms Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Texas?
You have until the 14th day after the notice of claim is delivered to pay. If you don't, the facility must advertise the sale. With newspaper ads, it can't sell until the 15th day after the first ad; with posted notices (allowed only where the county has no general-circulation newspaper), it can sell after the 10th day after posting Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[12] Justia (copy of Texas Property Code)[14].
Can a Texas storage facility send the lien notice by email?
Only if your written rental agreement says, underlined or in conspicuous bold print, that notice may be given by email if you choose to provide an email address. Otherwise the notice must be delivered in person or by verified mail, meaning any mailing method that gives evidence of mailing Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[13] Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[2].
Can I stop a storage auction in Texas?
Yes. Before the sale or other disposal, you can redeem your property by paying the facility the amount of the lien and its reasonable expenses under the storage lien law Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[7].
Do I get the extra money if my Texas storage unit sold for more than I owed?
The facility must send you written notice of the excess and hold it for you. You must ask for it within two years after the sale, or the facility owns it Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[17].
Can I live in my storage unit in Texas?
No. Texas law says a tenant may not use or allow the use of a self-service storage facility as a residence Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[8].
What can I do if a Texas facility broke the storage lien rules?
A person injured by a violation of chapter 59 may sue for damages under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act Texas Legislature, Texas Constitution and Statutes[4]. You can also file a complaint with the Texas Attorney General Office of the Texas Attorney General[19], and free legal help is listed below.
