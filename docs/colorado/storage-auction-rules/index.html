Colorado Storage Unit Auction Rules (C.R.S. 38-21.5): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Colorado storage auction rules: 30 days of default first, a written notice with 15 days to pay, ads, late fee limit, stopping the sale and leftover money.

Quick answer

  • The owner can't take any enforcement action until you've been in default continuously for 30 days Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
  • After that, you must get a written notice (in person, verified mail or email) giving you at least 15 days after delivery to pay Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
  • If an emailed sale notice gets no response or delivery confirmation, the owner must also send it by verified mail before the sale Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
  • The sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a local periodical, or in another commercially reasonable way Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
  • You can stop the sale any time before it happens by paying the lien plus the owner's reasonable expenses Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
  • A late fee of $20 or 20% of monthly rent, whichever is greater, is treated as reasonable, and it can only be part of the lien if your agreement states it Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[3].

Colorado's self-storage law is C.R.S. Title 38, Article 21.5 (sections 38-21.5-101 to 38-21.5-105) Colorado General Assembly (Office of Legislative Legal Services)[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant, and the owner includes the owner, operator, lessor or sublessor, an agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor or other charges, present or future, including late fees, plus expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably incurred in selling or disposing of it. The lien starts when your things are brought in and lasts while the owner keeps possession, until the default is fixed or the property is sold or disposed of Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[4]. "Default" means failing to do anything the law or your rental agreement requires on time Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2].

Before taking enforcement action, the owner must check whether a financing statement on the property has been filed with the Colorado Secretary of State, so a lender with an interest can be told too Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[4].

Your rental agreement must include a notice that everything stored will be sold or otherwise disposed of if no payment is received for a continuous 30-day period, and a provision asking you to disclose any lienholders with an interest in what you store Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[3]. The owner must also keep a notice posted in a prominent place in its office saying that stored articles will be sold or disposed of if charges go unpaid for 30 days Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[6].

Late fees

Your rental agreement may include a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent in full when due. A late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, for each late payment is treated as reasonable and not a penalty. The owner can't collect a late fee as part of the lien unless the amount is stated in your rental agreement or an addendum to it Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[3].

How notices are sent

The enforcement notice must be delivered in person, by verified mail or by email to your last-known address Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Verified mail is any U.S. Postal Service mailing method that provides evidence of mailing. Your last-known address is the postal or email address you gave in your latest rental agreement or in a later written change-of-address notice Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2], so keep both current.

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: 30 days of continuous default

The owner can't take any enforcement action until you've been in default continuously for 30 days Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].

Step 2: Written notice (at least 15 days to pay)

After 30 days of continuous default, the owner may begin enforcement only after notifying you in writing. The notice also goes to any lienholder the owner knows about through your rental agreement disclosure, a financing statement filed with the Secretary of State, or a written notice from the lienholder Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. The notice must include:

  • an itemized statement of the owner's claim, showing the amount due when the notice is sent and the date it became due
  • a brief, general description of the property subject to the lien, good enough to identify it (a locked or sealed trunk, box or similar container may be described as such without listing what's inside)
  • notice of any denial of access your rental agreement permits, with the name, street address and phone number of the owner or agent you can contact
  • a demand for payment within a stated time not less than 15 days after delivery of the notice
  • a conspicuous statement that unless the claim is paid in time, the property will be advertised and sold or otherwise disposed of at a specified time and place

If the owner emails a notice of a pending sale and gets no response, return receipt or delivery confirmation from that email address, it must send the sale notice by verified mail to your last-known postal address before going ahead Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].

Step 3: Advertising the sale

After your payment deadline passes, the owner must advertise the sale either by publishing an ad once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a periodical that circulates at least weekly in the county where the facility is, or in any other commercially reasonable manner. Advertising is considered commercially reasonable if at least 3 independent bidders (people not related to, or sharing a financial interest with, the owner or another bidder) attend the sale at the advertised time and place Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].

Step 4: The sale

The sale or other disposition must be held on an online auction website that customarily conducts public auctions, at the storage facility, or at the nearest suitable place to where the property is stored Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights even if the owner didn't follow the rules Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. That doesn't erase your rights against the owner if the law's requirements weren't met, the sale didn't match the notice, or there was a willful violation Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].

Vehicles and boats: if the lien is on a vehicle or watercraft and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days, the owner may have it towed by an independent towing carrier with current operating authority from the Colorado Public Utilities Commission. Once the tower takes possession, the owner isn't liable for it or for damage to it Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].

The owner isn't liable for identity theft or other harm from misuse of information in documents or electronic storage media that were part of your sold property, if it didn't actually know about them Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. If you can, get papers and devices with personal information out early.

How to stop the sale

Before the sale or other disposition, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the owner incurred under the sale procedure, and redeem your property. Once the owner receives that payment, it must return your property Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The law doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner may pay its lien from the sale money, subject to the rights of any prior lienholder. If the sale was made in good faith and in a reasonable way, the owner must hold any balance for you (or a lienholder or other person with an interest). If nobody claims the balance within 3 years of the sale date, it becomes the owner's property with no further recourse Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. So don't wait: ask the owner in writing for your balance as soon as you can, and keep a copy of your request.

Other Colorado rules

  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement limits the total value of what you store, that limit is treated as the maximum value of your stored property Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[3].
  • No living in units: no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2].
  • Warehouses: if the owner issues a warehouse receipt, bill of lading or other document of title for your property, the Uniform Commercial Code applies instead of this article Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2].
  • Other liens: the article doesn't limit liens created by special contract or other laws Colorado General Assembly (Office of Legislative Legal Services)[1].
  • Rent increases: the article doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Colorado General Assembly (Office of Legislative Legal Services)[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Colorado?
The law sets minimums, not a fixed date. Nothing can happen until you've been in default continuously for 30 days. Then you must get a written notice giving you at least 15 days after delivery to pay. After that deadline passes, the owner advertises the sale, either once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a local periodical or in another commercially reasonable way, and holds it at the time and place stated Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
Can a Colorado storage facility send auction notices by email?
Yes. The notice may be delivered in person, by verified mail or by email to your last-known address Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. But if the owner emails a notice of a pending sale and gets no response, return receipt or delivery confirmation from that email address, it must send the sale notice by verified mail to your last-known postal address before going ahead Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Your last-known address is the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep both current Public.Law (full text of C.R.S.)[2].
Can a Colorado storage facility lock me out?
Only if your rental agreement allows it. The written notice must tell you about any denial of access that your agreement permits, and give the name, street address and phone number of the owner or agent you can contact about it Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Check your agreement for the exact terms.
How do I stop a storage auction in Colorado?
Before the sale, pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the owner incurred under the sale procedure. The owner must then return your property Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
Can they tow my car or boat from a Colorado storage unit?
If the lien is on a vehicle or watercraft and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days, the owner may have it towed by an independent towing carrier with current operating authority from the Colorado Public Utilities Commission. Once the tower takes it, the owner isn't liable for it Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5].
What happens to the leftover money after a Colorado storage auction?
The owner may pay its lien from the sale money (subject to any prior lienholder) and must hold any balance for you or another person with an interest. If nobody claims the balance within 3 years of the sale date, it becomes the owner's property Justia (2025 Colorado Revised Statutes)[5]. Ask for it in writing as soon as you can.
