California Storage Unit Auction Rules (Bus. & Prof. Code 21700-21716): Timeline, Notices and Your Rights

How a California storage unit gets sold: the 14-day lien notice, the declaration in opposition, newspaper ads, late-fee caps and claiming leftover money.

Quick answer

California's self-storage lien law is the California Self-Service Storage Facility Act, Business and Professions Code sections 21700 to 21716 California Legislative Information[1]. In it, the renter is called the occupant and the facility is the owner California Legislative Information[2]. Below is a plain-English summary of the statute text. Your rental agreement can add terms, such as a limit on the value of what you store California Legislative Information[1], so read it alongside this page.

The facility's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor, late fees and other charges under the rental agreement, plus expenses needed to preserve, sell or dispose of the property California Legislative Information[3]. The law also says no occupant may use a storage facility for residential purposes California Legislative Information[2].

Two things in your rental agreement matter California Legislative Information[12]:

  • It must be in writing and state that your property is subject to a lien and may be sold if charges stay unpaid for 14 consecutive days.
  • It must ask for, and give space for, the name and mailing or email address of another person who will also get lien notices. If the agreement doesn't ask for that alternative contact, the chapter doesn't apply and its lien doesn't attach. If you simply chose not to give one, the owner's remedies are not affected.

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Preliminary lien notice (after 14 days unpaid)

If any rent or other charges stay unpaid for 14 consecutive days, the owner may send a preliminary lien notice to your last known address and to your alternative contact California Legislative Information[5]. It must go by certified mail, by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, or by email if you agreed to it California Legislative Information[5]. It must include California Legislative Information[5]:

  • an itemized statement of what's owed and when each amount became due
  • the date your right to use the space ends, at least 14 days after mailing, unless you pay everything first
  • a warning that you may be denied access after that date and that an owner's lien may then be imposed
  • the name, street address and phone number of the owner or its agent

Step 2: Lockout and notice of lien sale

If you haven't paid the full amount by the termination date, the lien attaches and the owner may deny you access, enter the space and move your property to safekeeping California Legislative Information[6]. It must then send you (and your alternative contact) a notice of lien sale by certified mail, first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, or agreed email. That notice must say California Legislative Information[6]:

  • your right to use the space has ended and you no longer have access
  • the current lien amount, and that it will keep growing if rent isn't paid
  • the property will be sold after a date at least 14 days from mailing, unless you return a declaration in opposition by certified mail
  • you can regain full use of the space by paying the full lien amount before that date
  • any excess sale money will be held for one year and then go to the county

It must also include a blank declaration in opposition to lien sale California Legislative Information[6].

Step 3: Advertising the sale

If no valid declaration comes back in time, the owner may sell after advertising California Legislative Information[7]. The ad must name the person whose goods are stored and the facility's name and location, and it must run in one of two ways California Legislative Information[8]:

  • once a week for two consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation, or
  • once in such a newspaper plus once on a public website that conducts or advertises online auctions, kept online for seven days before the sale

If there's no qualifying newspaper, the ad must be posted at least 10 days before the sale in at least six conspicuous places near the sale California Legislative Information[8].

Step 4: The sale

The sale must be conducted in a commercially reasonable manner, which includes an in-person auction or a sale on a public online auction website California Legislative Information[8]. A good-faith buyer takes the goods free of your rights even if the owner didn't follow the chapter California Legislative Information[11], so act before the sale.

Vehicles and boats: a lien on a registered vehicle or vessel is enforced under Civil Code section 3071 (vehicles) or Harbors and Navigation Code section 503 (vessels), not the storage sale steps above. The owner may also have it towed if charges are unpaid for 60 days, after mailing you at least 10 days' notice naming the towing company and where to redeem it California Legislative Information[4].

How to stop the sale

You have three options under the statute:

  • Pay the full lien amount before the date in the notice of lien sale, and you regain full use of the space California Legislative Information[6].
  • Return the declaration in opposition if you believe the lien isn't valid (for example, you already paid). Fill in every section, including a physical address and phone number where you can be served, sign it under penalty of perjury, and send it by certified mail so it arrives on or before the date in the notice California Legislative Information[6] California Legislative Information[7]. The owner then can't sell unless it wins in small claims or another court California Legislative Information[10]. You may owe court costs if the owner wins California Legislative Information[6].
  • Pay the lien plus one month's rent in advance before the sale if you claim a right to the goods. The goods are then held pending a court order, and if none is obtained within 30 days you must keep paying monthly rent or the owner may sell California Legislative Information[9].

Ask for an itemized payoff in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and get written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. If the sale date is close and you're unsure what to do, contact free legal help right away.

Leftover money from the sale

After deducting the lien and sale costs, the owner keeps any excess on your behalf. You, or someone with a court order against the property, can claim it within one year of the sale. After that, the owner pays what's left to the treasury of the county where the sale was held California Legislative Information[8]. Ask the facility in writing first; after a year, contact that county's treasurer.

Late fees

A California storage owner may charge a reasonable late fee only if California Legislative Information[15]:

  • rent is unpaid for at least 10 days after the due date
  • the fee amount is written in your rental agreement
  • it charges only one late fee per missed payment

The fee can't exceed $10 if monthly rent is $60 or less, $15 if rent is over $60 but under $100, or the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent if rent is $100 or more California Legislative Information[15].

Rent increases and new-agreement disclosures

The owner can change the terms of your agreement, including rent, after giving at least 30 days' written notice by personal delivery, first-class mail or agreed email. The new terms take effect if you keep storing there California Legislative Information[17]. For agreements first signed on or after January 1, 2026, the first page must clearly disclose the term and renewal, any promotional rate and how long it lasts, whether the rent can change and the maximum rent the owner could charge in the first 12 months, and every step to cancel California Legislative Information[16]. Property left after the rental ends

If your agreement ends and property is still in the unit, the owner must send written notice describing it, where to claim it, and a deadline at least 15 days after personal delivery or email (18 days if mailed). Claiming it within two days after the agreement ends can avoid storage costs California Legislative Information[13]. Unclaimed property must be sold at public sale, unless the owner reasonably believes it's worth less than $300, and unclaimed sale money goes to the county, where you can claim it within one year California Legislative Information[14].

If the facility broke the rules

You can file a consumer complaint with the California Attorney General California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General[18], and the free legal help above can tell you whether you have a claim.

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in California?
The process can start after 14 consecutive days of unpaid charges. The preliminary lien notice must give at least 14 days to pay, the notice of lien sale must give at least 14 more days, and then the sale must be advertised before it's held California Legislative Information[5] California Legislative Information[6] California Legislative Information[8].
Can I stop a storage auction in California?
Yes. You can regain full use of the space by paying the full lien amount before the date in the notice of lien sale California Legislative Information[6]. If you believe the lien isn't valid, you can sign and return the declaration in opposition by certified mail before that date; the owner then has to sue to enforce the lien instead of selling California Legislative Information[7] California Legislative Information[10].
Can a California storage facility send lien notices by email?
Only if the rental agreement says lien notices may be sent by email and you signed the agreement consenting to it. If the owner can't show you actually received the email, it must resend the notice by mail California Legislative Information[12].
What is the maximum storage late fee in California?
No late fee until rent is at least 10 days overdue, only one per missed payment, and the amount must be in your agreement. The cap is $10 if monthly rent is $60 or less, $15 if rent is over $60 but under $100, and the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent if rent is $100 or more California Legislative Information[15].
How do I get the leftover money after my California storage unit was sold?
The owner keeps any excess over the lien and sale costs on your behalf, and you can claim it within one year of the sale. After that the owner pays it to the treasury of the county where the sale was held California Legislative Information[8].
Can a California storage facility raise my rent?
It can change the terms, including rent, after at least 30 days' written notice; the new terms take effect if you keep storing there California Legislative Information[17]. For agreements first signed on or after January 1, 2026, the first page must also disclose any promotional rate and the maximum rent the owner could charge in the first 12 months California Legislative Information[16].
