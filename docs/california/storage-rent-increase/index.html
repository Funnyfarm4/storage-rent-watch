California Storage Rent Increases: The 30-Day Notice Rule

California storage rent increases need 30 days' written notice. How Bus. & Prof. Code 21715.5 works, new first-page disclosures, and what to do.

California's self-storage law is the California Self-Service Storage Facility Act, Business and Professions Code sections 21700 to 21716 California Legislative Information[1]. In it, the renter is the occupant and the facility is the owner California Legislative Information[2]. Below is a plain-English summary of what the statute says about rent and term changes. Your rental agreement can add terms California Legislative Information[5], so read it alongside this page.

The 30-day notice rule

The owner may change the terms of your rental agreement, including rent, after giving you at least 30 days' written notice California Legislative Information[8]. Notice must be given by one of these methods California Legislative Information[8]:

  • personal delivery
  • first-class mail, postage prepaid, to your last known address
  • email, but only under the email rules in section 21712 (the agreement must allow email notices and you must have signed consenting) California Legislative Information[4]

Once at least 30 days have passed from the date of service, the notice itself establishes the new terms, rents and conditions if you keep storing personal property at the facility after it takes effect California Legislative Information[8]. Leaving before then is how you avoid the new rent.

The chapter does not set a dollar or percentage cap on increases, and it does not limit how often rent may change California Legislative Information[1] California Legislative Information[8]. Those details, if any, come from your agreement California Legislative Information[5].

New first-page disclosures (agreements on or after January 1, 2026)

For a rental agreement initially entered into on or after January 1, 2026, the owner must put clear disclosures on the first page, in larger type than the surrounding text and set off by contrast or marks California Legislative Information[7]. Those disclosures must cover California Legislative Information[7]:

  • the initial length and renewal term
  • whether you received a promotional or discounted rental fee, and if so how long it lasts
  • whether the rental fee can change and, if so, the maximum rental fee the owner could charge during the first 12 months after the agreement date
  • every required step to terminate and avoid future fees or charges, including removing all personal property from the space
  • the owner's contact information

If you are shopping for a unit, ask for those numbers in writing before you sign, even when the agreement is older than that date.

Why the price went up: teaser rates

Many facilities advertise a low first month or first few months. Once that promotional rate ends, the rent can rise under the notice rule above. Before you sign anywhere, ask two questions and get the answers in writing:

  • What will my monthly rent be after any special or introductory rate ends?
  • How often can the rent change, and how much notice will I get?

For agreements signed on or after January 1, 2026, the first-page disclosures above should answer both California Legislative Information[7].

Keep paying while you sort it out

Don't withhold rent to protest an increase. Unpaid charges can start the lien process after 14 consecutive days, and the facility can later deny access and sell your property California Legislative Information[3]. Late fees also apply once rent is at least 10 days overdue: only one fee per missed payment, the amount must be in your agreement, and the cap is $10 if monthly rent is $60 or less, $15 if rent is over $60 but under $100, or the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent if rent is $100 or more California Legislative Information[6].

For the full lien-sale timeline, declaration in opposition and leftover-money rules, see California storage auction rules. If you already have a notice of lien sale, see how to stop a storage auction in California.

Negotiate, shrink or move

Ask the manager, politely and in writing, to lower or delay the increase. It helps to have:

  • quotes from two or three nearby facilities for the same size unit
  • your payment history (on time every month is your best argument)
  • a clear ask, such as keeping your current rate for a few more months

If the facility says no, ask whether a smaller unit at the same location would cost less. You can also:

  • Downsize. Clear out what you don't need and move to a smaller unit.
  • Move to a cheaper facility. Add up moving costs (truck, time, a new lock, any admin fee) against what you'll save each month.
  • Try peer-to-peer storage. Neighbor is a marketplace where hosts rent out space such as garages, basements and driveways Neighbor[10]. Compare real quotes and read the host's terms.

If you think the facility broke the rules

If a facility raised rent without the required 30-day written notice, skipped first-page disclosures on a new agreement, or charged late fees your agreement doesn't allow, you can file a consumer complaint with the California Attorney General California Department of Justice, Office of the Attorney General[9]. Frequently asked questions

How much notice does a California storage facility need before raising rent?
At least 30 days' written notice. The owner can change the terms of the rental agreement, including rent, after giving notice by personal delivery, first-class mail to your last known address, or agreed email. The new terms take effect after at least 30 days from service if you keep storing there California Legislative Information[8].
Is there a cap on how much California storage rent can go up?
No. The California Self-Service Storage Facility Act does not set a dollar or percentage cap on rent increases, and it does not limit how often rent can change. It requires the 30-day written notice, and for agreements first signed on or after January 1, 2026 it requires first-page disclosures about promotional rates and the maximum rent in the first 12 months California Legislative Information[1] California Legislative Information[8] California Legislative Information[7]. Your rental agreement can still set its own terms California Legislative Information[5].
What must a new California storage rental agreement disclose about rent?
For agreements initially entered into on or after January 1, 2026, the first page must clearly disclose the term and renewal, whether you got a promotional or discounted fee and how long it lasts, whether rent can change and the maximum fee the owner could charge in the first 12 months, every step to terminate and avoid future charges (including removing your property), and the owner's contact information California Legislative Information[7].
Should I stop paying if I think a California storage rent increase is unfair?
No. Unpaid rent can start the lien process after 14 consecutive days and lead to lockout and a sale. Late fees also apply once rent is at least 10 days overdue, with caps based on your monthly rent California Legislative Information[3] California Legislative Information[6]. Pay, negotiate, and move if you need to. See California storage auction rules.
Can a California storage facility email a rent-increase notice?
Yes, but only by email under the same rules that allow email for lien notices: the rental agreement must say notices may be sent by email and you must have signed consenting to email California Legislative Information[8] California Legislative Information[4]. Otherwise the owner should use personal delivery or first-class mail to your last known address California Legislative Information[8].
What else should I check before I sign a California storage unit?
Ask what the rent will be after any promotional rate ends, how often it can change, and how much notice you will get. For agreements signed on or after January 1, 2026, that information must appear clearly on the first page, including the maximum rent in the first 12 months California Legislative Information[7]. Also read the late-fee and lien language California Legislative Information[6] California Legislative Information[4].
