Washington Storage Unit Auction Rules (RCW 19.150): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Washington storage auction rules: 14-day preliminary notice, final lien-sale notice, 42-day / 14-day sale floor, $300 value split, late fees, and how to redeem.

Quick answer

  • After 14 consecutive days of unpaid rent or charges, the owner may send a preliminary lien notice by first-class mail or (if your agreement allows) email Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5].
  • If you don't pay by the date in that notice, the lien attaches, the owner may lock you out, enter the space, inventory and move your property to safekeeping, and must send a final lien-sale notice Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
  • A sale date in the final notice must be at least 14 days after that notice is sent, and at least 42 days after any unpaid rent or charges first became due, whichever is later Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
  • Property valued at $300 or more must be sold in a commercially reasonable public sale; under $300 may be disposed of in a reasonable manner (donation, trash hauler, or similar) Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2].
  • A late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is deemed reasonable if the rental agreement allows late fees Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[12].
  • Excess sale proceeds stay with the owner for six months, then go to the state as abandoned property under chapter 63.30 RCW Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].

Washington's self-storage law is the Washington Self-Service Storage Facility Act, chapter 19.150 RCW Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1]. In this chapter the renter is the occupant, and owner includes the operator, lessor, sublessor, agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too, and the chapter does not wipe out other rights the parties create so long as they don't conflict with it Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor, late fees, and costs of the sale under the rental agreement, plus expenses needed to preserve, sell, or dispose of the property. The lien is enforced only as this chapter allows Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[3].

A lien on a motor vehicle or boat that has already attached and is shown on the title documents has priority over the facility's chapter 19.150 lien Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[3].

Your written rental agreement must say your property will be subject to a lien claim and may be sold if rent or other charges stay unpaid for fourteen consecutive days, and that those steps are authorized by this chapter. The agreement must also ask for, and leave space for, another person's name and address for notices. Notices go to your address and that alternative address when both are provided. Not naming an alternative contact does not cut off the owner's remedies Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[11].

No occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2]. The owner is not required to insure your property Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].

Late fees and lockouts

Any late fee must be in the rental agreement (or an addendum). The owner may charge a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent when due. A late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rental amount, whichever is greater, is deemed reasonable and is not treated as a penalty Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[12].

After six consecutive days unpaid, if the rental agreement allows it, the owner may deny access to the space Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[4].

Chapter 19.150 does not set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Preliminary lien notice (after 14 days unpaid)

When any part of the rent or other charges stays unpaid for fourteen consecutive days, the owner may terminate your right to use the space by sending a preliminary lien notice to your last known address and to the alternative address, by first-class mail or by email if the email rules below are met Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5].

That notice must include Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5]:

  • an itemized statement of the owner's claim (sums due now and when they became due)
  • a statement that your right to use the space will terminate on a specified date not less than fourteen days after the notice is sent, unless you pay all sums due and to become due by that date
  • a warning that you may be denied access after that date if you don't pay, and that an owner's lien under RCW 19.150.020 may be imposed
  • the name, street address, and telephone number of the owner or designated agent you can contact

RCW 19.150.050 supplies a sample form that satisfies those requirements Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[6].

Email for this notice is allowed only if you expressly agree, the rental agreement says in bold type that notices will be given by email, the owner gives you the sending address and tells you to allow it through filters, and the owner notifies you before any change to that sending address Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5].

Step 2: Lien attaches and final lien-sale notice

If the total due is still unpaid on the date in the preliminary notice, the proposed lien attaches that day. The owner may then deny access, enter the space, inventory the goods, and move property to safekeeping Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].

The owner must give you a notice of final lien sale (or final notice of disposition) by personal service, verified mail, or email to your last known address and alternative address or email. Verified mail means any USPS method that provides evidence of mailing Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2]. If the owner emails that notice and gets no reply or delivery receipt, it must send a second notice by verified mail to your last known postal address Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].

The final notice must state all of the following Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7]:

  • that your right to use the space has ended and you no longer have access
  • that the property is subject to a lien, and the amount accrued and to accrue before the sale date
  • that property other than personal papers and personal photographs may be sold after a specified date that is not less than 14 days from the last date the final notice is sent, or a minimum of 42 days after unpaid rent or other charges first became due, whichever is later, unless the lien amount is paid (the owner is not required to sell within any maximum number of days after charges first became due)
  • that if total value in the space is less than $300, the owner may dispose of the property in any reasonable manner instead of selling it (subject to who may not acquire it)
  • that after sale or other disposition, the owner will send you an accounting at your last known address and alternative address
  • that stored vehicles, watercraft, trailers, RVs, or campers may be towed or removed instead of sold under RCW 19.150.160
  • that excess proceeds over the lien and reasonable costs of sale are kept by the owner and may be reclaimed for six months from the sale, then turned over to the state as abandoned property under chapter 63.30 RCW
  • that personal papers and photographs are kept for six months from sale or other disposition, then may be disposed of in a reasonable manner
  • that you have no right to repurchase property sold at the lien sale

Email rules for the final notice match the preliminary-notice email rules Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].

Step 3: Sale or other disposition

After the time in the final notice expires, property other than personal papers and photographs may be sold or disposed of as follows Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[8] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9]:

  1. Value $300 or more: sale in a commercially reasonable manner (a public sale on or off the facility, as a single lot or in parcels). If five or more bidders attend, the proceeds are deemed commercially reasonable Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].
  2. Value under $300: dispose of in a reasonable manner (donation to a nonprofit, removal by a trash hauler or recycler, or another method the owner reasonably chooses) Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].

Personal papers and photographs that aren't reclaimed within six months after sale or other disposition may then be disposed of in a reasonable manner Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].

No employee or owner, and no family member of an employee or owner, may acquire the sold or disposed property (including those papers and photographs) Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].

Chapter 19.150 does not require a newspaper advertisement of the sale Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].

Vehicles and boats: if you've been in default 60 or more days and the stored property is a vehicle, watercraft, trailer, recreational vehicle, or camper, the owner may have it towed instead of sold. Before the tow, the owner must give you notice naming the towing company and its contact information. Once a third party has the property, the owner is not liable for later damage Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[13].

A good-faith purchaser of goods disposed of under the $300-or-more sale rules takes free of rights of people against whom the lien was claimed, even if the owner didn't fully comply with the chapter Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].

How to stop the sale

Pay the lien amount before the sale date in the final notice. The final notice itself says the property may be sold unless that amount is paid Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].

Separately, before any sale, a person claiming a right to the property may pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus one month's rent in advance. Then the property may not be sold and must be held pending a court order on disposition. If no order arrives within 30 days, that claimant must keep paying the monthly rent for the space; if rent stops, the owner may sell or dispose of the property again under RCW 19.150.080 Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[10].

Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. Chapter 19.150 doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, use the contact details required in the notices Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

For property sold at $300 or more, proceeds go first to costs of the sale, then to the lien. The owner keeps any excess for you. You (or anyone with a court order or other judicial process against the property) may claim the excess, or enough of it to cover that claim, within six months of the sale date Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9]. The owner may keep interest earned on excess proceeds until they are claimed or turned over to the state Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].

After six months, unclaimed excess is turned over to the state as abandoned property under chapter 63.30 RCW Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9]. Search and claim later through the Washington Department of Revenue's Unclaimed Property program and claim search Washington Department of Revenue[14] Washington Department of Revenue[15].

If the owner broke the rules

Chapter 19.150 does not create a special statutory damages amount for noncompliance the way some other states do. Other rights between creditor and debtor remain available Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1]. Keep copies of every notice and payment, and talk with free legal help if something looks wrong.

Other Washington rules

  • No living in units: no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2].
  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement sets a limit on the value of property you may store, that limit is the maximum value for the owner's liability only Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1].
  • Rent increases: chapter 19.150 doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Washington?
After 14 consecutive days unpaid, the owner may send a preliminary lien notice giving you at least 14 more days to pay. If you miss that deadline, it sends a final lien-sale notice. The sale date must be at least 14 days after that final notice is sent, and at least 42 days after unpaid charges first became due, whichever is later Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
Can a Washington storage facility lock me out?
Yes. After six consecutive days unpaid, if your rental agreement says so, the owner may deny access Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[4]. After the preliminary-notice payment date passes without payment, the lien attaches and the owner may deny access, enter the space, inventory goods, and move property to safekeeping Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
Can a Washington storage facility email auction notices?
Only if you expressly agree, the rental agreement says in bold type that notices go by email, the owner gives you the sending address (and tells you about address changes), and you are directed to allow that address through filters. For the final lien-sale notice, if email gets no reply or delivery receipt, the owner must send a second notice by verified mail to your last known postal address Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[5] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
Does Washington require newspaper ads before a storage auction?
Chapter 19.150 does not require a newspaper advertisement. Property worth $300 or more must be sold in a commercially reasonable manner, defined as a public sale (on or off site, as a single lot or in parcels). If five or more bidders attend, the proceeds are deemed commercially reasonable Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[2] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9].
What happens to leftover money after a Washington storage auction?
After costs of sale and the lien, the owner keeps excess proceeds for you (or someone with a court order against the property) for six months from the sale date, and may keep interest earned on that money. After six months, unused excess goes to the state as abandoned property under chapter 63.30 RCW Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7] Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[9]. Search later at the Department of Revenue's unclaimed property site Washington Department of Revenue[14] Washington Department of Revenue[15].
What happens to my car or boat in a Washington storage unit?
A motor-vehicle or boat title lien that already attached has priority over the facility's chapter 19.150 lien Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[3]. If you've been in default 60 or more days, the owner may have a vehicle, watercraft, trailer, RV, or camper towed instead of sold, after notice naming the towing company Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[13]. The final lien-sale notice must also warn that stored vehicles and similar property may be towed under that section Washington State Legislature (app.leg.wa.gov)[7].
