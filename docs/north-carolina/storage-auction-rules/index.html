North Carolina Storage Unit Auction Rules (N.C. Gen. Stat. 44A-40): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

How a storage unit gets sold in North Carolina: 15 days unpaid, a notice with a 10-day hearing right, 20 days' sale notice, a 5-day ad, and leftover money.

  • The owner can start enforcing its lien once rent and other charges stay unpaid for 15 days after rent was due North Carolina General Assembly[5].
  • The first lien notice gives you 10 days from the date it's mailed to ask, by registered or certified mail, for a court hearing before any sale North Carolina General Assembly[5].

North Carolina's self-storage law is Article 4 of Chapter 44A of the General Statutes, §§ 44A-40 to 44A-46 North Carolina General Assembly[1]. The sale process is in § 44A-43 and your right to redeem is in § 44A-44 North Carolina General Assembly[5] North Carolina General Assembly[6]. In North Carolina law the renter is called the occupant and the facility acting on its lien is the lienor North Carolina General Assembly[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement can create other liens by contract North Carolina General Assembly[8], so read it too.

The owner's lien

The facility has a lien on all personal property stored there for rent, expenses needed to preserve the property, and expenses reasonably incurred selling or disposing of it. The lien doesn't come ahead of a security interest that was already perfected when you stored the property, and the owner must search the Secretary of State's online records for financing statements in your name to find such lenders North Carolina General Assembly[3].

The lien ends when the owner gives up possession of the property, or when you (or a lender) tender the rent plus the owner's preservation expenses before the sale North Carolina General Assembly[4].

"Personal property" means movable property not attached to land, including goods, merchandise, household items and watercraft North Carolina General Assembly[2]. Unless your rental agreement says otherwise, care and control of your things stays with you until they're sold, and the owner is liable for damage caused by its own or its employees' intentional acts or negligence North Carolina General Assembly[7].

When enforcement can start

If rent and other charges stay unpaid for 15 days after rent was due, the owner may enforce its lien by a public sale or other disposition. The owner can also sue you for the rent at any time after it's due North Carolina General Assembly[5].

You (or a lender) can go to court at any time after the owner asserts the lien and ask for immediate possession of the property. Before possession is granted, you must pay the lien amount to the clerk of the court where the case is pending, or post a bond for double that amount. The clerk then orders the owner to give the property back North Carolina General Assembly[5].

Article 4 itself doesn't set a late-fee cap or a rent-increase notice period North Carolina General Assembly[1], so check your rental agreement for both.

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: The lien notice and your hearing right

After the 15 days pass, the owner must send a notice to you at your last known address, and to anyone with a security interest in the property if reasonably ascertainable North Carolina General Assembly[5]. Your last known address is the mailing or email address in your latest rental agreement, or one you later gave in a written change-of-address notice North Carolina General Assembly[2], so keep it current.

The notice counts as delivered once it's properly addressed and mailed first-class, or once it's sent by verified email, but email is allowed only if you chose email notice in your rental agreement North Carolina General Assembly[5]. "Verified email" means email sent to an address the sender has checked by reasonable means is working North Carolina General Assembly[2].

The notice must North Carolina General Assembly[5]:

  • say a lien is being asserted against your property for rental charges, give the amount, and say the owner intends to sell or otherwise dispose of the property
  • give a brief, general description of the property (a locked or sealed trunk or box can be described as just that)
  • tell you about your right to a judicial hearing on whether the lien is valid before any sale
  • say you have 10 days from the date the notice was mailed to ask for that hearing by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested

If you don't ask for a hearing within those 10 days, you've waived it, and the owner may go ahead with a public sale. When the 10 days end, your tenancy is terminated and the owner may move your property to another place of safekeeping North Carolina General Assembly[5].

If you do ask in time, the owner can sell only under an order from a court North Carolina General Assembly[5].

Step 2: The sale notice

At least 20 days before the sale, the owner must send notice by certified mail to you at your last known address (or by verified email, if you chose email in your agreement), and by certified mail to any secured lender it can reasonably find. Certified mail counts as delivered when it's mailed, and verified email when it's sent North Carolina General Assembly[5].

Step 3: Advertising the sale

At least 5 days before the sale, the owner must publish notice of it, either in a newspaper of general circulation in the county where the sale will be held or in another commercially reasonable way. If at least three independent bidders attend the sale at the advertised time and place, the advertising is treated as commercially reasonable North Carolina General Assembly[5].

Step 4: The sale

The sale must be commercially reasonable and can be held on a publicly accessible online auction website. A live auction must be held at the facility, the nearest suitable place where the property is stored, or in the county where the rental was contracted, not on a Sunday, and between 9:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. The owner may bid at its own sale North Carolina General Assembly[5].

A good-faith buyer who didn't know of any defect in the sale takes the property free of your rights North Carolina General Assembly[6], which is why it matters to act before the sale date.

Cars, boats and trailers: for a motor vehicle, the owner must notify the Division of Motor Vehicles after the 15 days pass, and the DMV mails a notice to the title holder and to you. You then have 10 days from receiving it to tell the DMV by certified mail, return receipt requested, that you want a hearing. If rent stays unpaid for 60 days, the owner may instead have a motor vehicle, watercraft or trailer towed, and isn't liable for a towed motor vehicle once the tow company takes it North Carolina General Assembly[5].

How to stop the sale

Before the sale or other disposition, you can pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus the owner's reasonable expenses for preserving the property, and redeem it. Once paid, the owner must return your property and has no further claim against it for that lien North Carolina General Assembly[6].

A partial payment doesn't stop the sale. It doesn't satisfy the lien or stop or delay the sale unless the owner agrees to that in a writing it signs North Carolina General Assembly[6]. If you work out a payment plan, get it in writing, signed by the owner.

If you think the lien amount is wrong, use your hearing right in Step 1 and mail your request within 10 days of the notice's mailing date North Carolina General Assembly[5]. If the deadline is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

Sale money is applied in this order: reasonable sale expenses, then any secured lender whose interest was perfected when you stored the property, then the facility's lien. Any balance goes to you or whoever is lawfully entitled to it. If you can't be found, the balance is paid to the clerk of superior court of the county where the sale took place, who holds it for you North Carolina General Assembly[6].

So if your unit was sold, ask the facility in writing for the sale results and any balance, and if it says the money went to the court, contact the clerk of superior court in that county. You can also search for money in your name on NCCash, the North Carolina Department of State Treasurer's official unclaimed property site North Carolina Department of State Treasurer[9].

If the owner fails to substantially comply with § 44A-44, it's liable to you for $100 plus reasonable attorney's fees awarded by the court, on top of any actual damages North Carolina General Assembly[6]. To complain about a facility, you can contact the North Carolina Department of Justice's consumer protection division North Carolina Department of Justice[10].

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in North Carolina?
The owner can start the process once rent stays unpaid for 15 days after it was due. You then get a notice with 10 days from mailing to request a court hearing, the owner must send a sale notice at least 20 days before the sale, and the sale must be advertised at least 5 days before it North Carolina General Assembly[5].
Can I stop a North Carolina storage auction by asking for a hearing?
You can ask for a judicial hearing on whether the lien is valid. Send your request to the owner by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, within 10 days from the date the notice was mailed, and say whether you want to contest the sale. If you ask in time, the owner can sell only under a court order North Carolina General Assembly[5].
Will a partial payment stop my storage unit from being sold in North Carolina?
Not by itself. A partial payment doesn't satisfy the lien or stop or delay the sale unless the owner agrees to that in a writing it signs North Carolina General Assembly[6]. To redeem your things, pay the full lien amount plus the owner's reasonable preservation expenses before the sale North Carolina General Assembly[6].
Can a North Carolina storage facility send the lien notice by email?
Only if you chose email notice in your rental agreement. Then the notice can go by verified email, meaning email to an address the sender has reasonably checked is working North Carolina General Assembly[2] North Carolina General Assembly[5]. Otherwise the first notice goes by first-class mail and the sale notice by certified mail North Carolina General Assembly[5].
What happens to my car or boat in a North Carolina storage unit?
For a motor vehicle, the owner must notify the Division of Motor Vehicles, which mails a notice giving the title holder and you 10 days from receipt to ask for a hearing. If rent stays unpaid for 60 days, the owner may instead have a motor vehicle, watercraft or trailer towed North Carolina General Assembly[5].
