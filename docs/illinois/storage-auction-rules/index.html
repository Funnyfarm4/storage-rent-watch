Illinois Storage Unit Auction Rules (770 ILCS 95): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

How a storage unit gets sold in Illinois under 770 ILCS 95: the 14-day notice, two weeks of newspaper ads, the 15-day rule, late fees, and the 1-year rule.

Illinois's self-storage law is the Self-Service Storage Facility Act, 770 ILCS 95 Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[1]. The lien sale process is in Section 4 Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. In Illinois law the renter is called the occupant Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement can add terms of its own, so read it too.

The owner's lien

The facility has a lien on everything you store for rent, labor or other charges related to the property, plus expenses needed to preserve it and expenses reasonably incurred selling or disposing of it under the Act. The lien attaches the day your property is brought to the facility Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[3].

"Default" means failing to do anything your rental agreement or the Act requires Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[2]. If you never signed a written agreement the owner gave you, continuing to use the space counts as accepting it Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[2].

Late fees

A late fee can only be charged if rent stays unpaid for at least 5 days after the due date in your agreement, and only if the fee amount and conditions are written in the agreement or an addendum. A late fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is deemed reasonable Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[7]. The owner can also charge reasonable rent collection and lien enforcement expenses, but they must be shown on an itemized list available to you Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[7]. Ask for that list if your balance looks higher than expected.

Lockout

If your rental agreement allows it, the owner can deny you access, for example by over-locking your unit. Until a sale, your property stays in your care and control, and rent and other charges keep adding up while you're locked out Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: The lien notice

The notice must be delivered in person, or sent by verified mail (any mail service that gives evidence of mailing) or by email to your last known address Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4] Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[2]. Your last known address is the mailing or email address in your latest rental agreement, or one you later gave the owner in a written change-of-address notice Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[2], so keep it up to date.

A mailed notice counts as delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service, properly addressed and with postage paid. An email counts as delivered when the owner gets a delivery receipt; if no receipt comes back, the notice counts as delivered when it's sent by verified mail to your last known mailing address Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].

The notice must include Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]:

  • an itemized statement of the claim, showing the amount due and the date it became due
  • the facility's name, address and phone number, the date, time, location and manner of the sale (including the bidding website if the sale is online), and your name and unit number
  • if you've been denied access, the name, street address and phone number of the person you can contact to respond
  • a demand for payment within a time not less than 14 days after delivery
  • a conspicuous statement that unless you pay in time, the property will be advertised and sold or otherwise disposed of at a specified time and place

Step 2: Advertising the sale

After the notice deadline passes, the sale must be advertised once a week for two consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation where the facility is located. The ad must give the facility's name, address and phone number, the date, time, location and manner of sale, and your name and unit number Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].

If there's no such newspaper where the facility is located, the ad must instead be posted in at least 6 conspicuous places in the neighborhood at least 10 days before the sale Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].

Step 3: The sale

The sale can't take place sooner than 15 days after the first publication Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. It must match the terms of the notice, and it must be held at the facility or the nearest suitable place; a sale on a publicly accessible website counts as held at the facility Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. If 3 or more bidders unrelated to the owner attend, the sale and its proceeds are deemed commercially reasonable Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. A good-faith buyer gets the property even if the owner didn't follow every rule Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4], which is why it matters to act before the sale date.

Vehicles, trailers and boats: if charges stay unpaid, or you're in default, for 60 days, the owner may have the vehicle, trailer or watercraft towed. Before selling a titled item like a vehicle or boat, the owner must check with the Secretary of State and notify every title holder or lienholder it finds Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].

How to stop the sale

Any time before the sale, you can pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses incurred under the Act, and redeem your property. Once the owner gets that payment, it must return your property Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.

If you think the amount is wrong, ask for the itemized statement and the list of collection expenses Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4] Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[7]. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner can pay itself what it's owed from the sale proceeds, but must hold any balance for delivery to you on demand. Illinois is stricter than many states here: if you don't claim the balance within one year of the sale, it becomes the owner's property and you lose the right to it Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4]. Unlike some states, the Act doesn't send this money to a state unclaimed property office, so ask the facility directly, in writing, as soon as you learn your unit was sold.

To complain about a facility, you can contact the Illinois Attorney General's Consumer Protection division Illinois Attorney General[8].

If the facility ends your rental

The owner can end or not renew your rental agreement by written notice delivered in person or by verified mail. The notice must give you at least 14 days after delivery to remove everything. Until then the owner can limit access to removing your things during normal business hours, and it may dispose of anything left after the date in the notice Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[5].

Value limits in your lease

If your rental agreement caps the value of what you can store, the cap counts as the maximum value of your property, but only if that clause is printed in bold type or underlined Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[6]. Only the occupant named on the last rental agreement can sue for damages for a violation of the Act Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[6].

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Illinois?
The notice must give you at least 14 days after delivery to pay. After that, the sale must be advertised once a week for two consecutive weeks, and the sale can't be held sooner than 15 days after the first ad Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].
Can an Illinois storage facility send the lien notice by email?
Yes. The notice can be delivered in person, by verified mail, or by email to your last known address. An emailed notice counts as delivered when the owner gets a delivery receipt; if no receipt comes back, the notice counts as delivered when it's sent to you by verified mail at your last known mailing address Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].
What is the late fee limit for storage units in Illinois?
A late fee can't be charged unless rent is unpaid for at least 5 days after the due date, and the fee and its conditions must be stated in your rental agreement or an addendum. A fee of $20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is deemed reasonable. Collection and lien enforcement expenses can be added, but they must be on an itemized list available to you Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[7].
Can an Illinois storage auction be held online?
Yes. The sale must be held at the facility or the nearest suitable place, and a sale on a publicly accessible online website counts as being held at the facility Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[4].
Can I live in my storage unit in Illinois?
No. Illinois law says no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes Illinois General Assembly (ilga.gov)[5].
