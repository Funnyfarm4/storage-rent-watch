Pennsylvania Storage Unit Auction Rules (12 Pa.C.S. Ch. 56): Timeline, Notices and Your Rights

How a Pennsylvania storage unit gets sold under the new 12 Pa.C.S. Chapter 56: lockout after 10 days, the 30-day notice, ads, late fees and leftover money.

Pennsylvania's self-storage lien rules now live in Title 12, Chapter 56 of the Pennsylvania Consolidated Statutes ("Self-Service Storage Facilities"). Act 51 of 2025 added the chapter, repealed the 1982 Self-Service Storage Facility Act, and set the chapter to take effect 60 days after it was signed on November 24, 2025 Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[1] Pennsylvania General Assembly[17]. The act says most wording changes are just style, but the lockout and towing rules, the leftover-money rule, the unsigned-agreement rule and the termination rule are new or changed Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[1]. If you see older guides citing "73 P.S. § 1901," that's the repealed law.

In the chapter, the renter is the occupant, the facility is the owner (which includes its agents, managers and employees), and your unit is the leased space Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[2]. Below is a plain-English summary of the statute text. Read your own rental agreement too, because the chapter lets the owner and occupant add rights and duties by contract Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[16].

The facility's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor, late fees and other charges for storing it, plus expenses to preserve it and reasonable expenses of selling or disposing of it. The lien attaches the day your property goes in, and your rental agreement must tell you about it in bold type Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4]. It's superior to other liens, except one that existed before the property was placed at the facility Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].

Late fees

  • A late fee of $20 a month or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is treated as reasonable and not a penalty Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].
  • The owner can't charge a late fee unless the rental agreement discloses the amount and when it's charged Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].
  • No late fee may be charged if you pay rent and other charges in full by the fifth day after the due date Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Default, then lockout after 10 days

"Default" means failing to meet an obligation on time under the chapter or your rental agreement Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[2]. After you've been in default continuously for at least 10 days, the owner may deny you access to your unit Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5].

Step 2: After 30 days, lien enforcement can start

The owner can't start enforcing its lien until you've been in default continuously for at least 30 days. At that point it may also enter your unit and move your things to other suitable storage while the sale is pending Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5]. A motor vehicle, trailer or watercraft can be towed after 60 days of continuous default Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5].

Step 3: The written notice of default

The owner must give you written notice of the default, and of any other action it takes with your property, by personal service, verified mail, certified mail (return receipt requested) or email, sent to your last known address Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6]. Verified mail is any USPS or private delivery method that gives proof of mailing Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[2]. Notice counts as served once it's mailed, properly addressed and paid, or emailed to an address you provided Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6].

Email only works if your agreement (or a later change to it) says email is an authorized way to notify you, you affirmatively agreed to electronic contact and to tell the owner if your email changes, and that consent is printed in bold or underlined in the agreement Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6]. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your agreement, or one you later gave in a written change-of-address notice Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[2], so update it in writing if you move.

The notice must include Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6]:

  • an itemized statement of the claim, showing the amount due and when it became due
  • a demand for payment within a stated time of at least 30 days after the notice date
  • a statement that the contents of your unit are subject to the owner's lien
  • the name, street address and phone number of the owner or its agent you can contact
  • a bold, conspicuous statement that unless you pay in time and at the stated place, your property will be advertised for sale or otherwise disposed of at a stated time and place, not less than 30 days after the notice date
  • a statement that you've been locked out or that your property was moved, if the owner did either

Step 4: Advertising

After the payment deadline in the notice passes, the owner must advertise the sale either twice in a local newspaper of general circulation, or once in that newspaper and on a public website that regularly advertises or runs online sales of personal property Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7]. The ad must say your unit's contents will be sold to satisfy the lien, and give the facility address, the unit number or description, your name, and the time, place and manner of sale Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7].

If there's no local newspaper of general circulation, the owner must post the ad in at least six conspicuous places in the neighborhood at least 10 days before the sale Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7]. An owner may also run a backup ad online; if the newspaper fails to print on time, the online ad counts from the date it's posted, and the owner must keep proof for a year Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7].

Step 5: The sale

The sale can't happen sooner than 10 days after the first publication or posting Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7]. It may be held at the facility, online, or at the nearest suitable place to where the property is stored Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[8], and it must match the terms in the notice. If an advertised sale doesn't go through, the owner must tell you in writing about any other disposition Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[10]. A good-faith buyer gets clear title even if the owner didn't follow the chapter Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[11], which is why acting before the sale matters.

How to stop the sale

Before the sale or other disposition, you can pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus other reasonable expenses under the chapter and redeem your property. Once you do, the owner must give your property back Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[9]. Practical steps:

  1. Ask for an itemized payoff amount in writing, and compare each charge with your rental agreement and the late-fee rule above Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].
  2. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt plus written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
  3. If you think the bill is wrong or the notice didn't follow the rules, get free legal help right away. Chapter 56 doesn't set out a special court process for disputing the lien, so a lawyer can tell you your options.

Leftover money from the sale

The owner may pay its lien from the sale proceeds but must hold any balance for delivery to you on demand. If you don't claim it within six months of the sale, it's treated as abandoned and unclaimed property, and the owner must report and pay it to the State Treasurer Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[12]. After that, search your name on Pennsylvania's official unclaimed property site Pennsylvania Treasury[18]. Ask the facility in writing what the sale brought in and where any balance went.

Other Pennsylvania rules to know

  • No living in units: an occupant may not use a storage facility for residential purposes Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[3].
  • Unsigned agreements still count: if the owner delivers a written rental agreement by hand, first-class mail or email and you don't sign it within 30 days, paying rent or continuing to use the unit counts as accepting it Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[14].
  • Ending your rental: an owner can terminate or not renew your agreement with written notice in person, by email or by verified mail, giving you at least 14 days to remove everything. Property left after that date may be disposed of Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[15].
  • Other people's property: your agreement must ask you to tell the owner about anything in the unit that isn't yours, and a value limit in the agreement is binding if it's in bold or underlined Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[13].
  • Rent increases: Chapter 56 has no rent-increase notice rule, so your agreement controls Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[16]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Pennsylvania?
The facility can't start enforcing its lien until you've been in default for at least 30 days in a row Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5]. The default notice must give you at least 30 days to pay and set a sale or disposal date no sooner than 30 days after the notice date Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6]. After that deadline passes, the sale must be advertised, and it can't happen sooner than 10 days after the first ad or posting Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[7]. Check your notice for the actual dates.
Can a Pennsylvania storage facility lock me out for late rent?
Yes. Once you've been in default continuously for at least 10 days, the owner may deny you access to your unit. If it does, the default notice must say so Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5] Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6].
Can the storage lien notice be sent by email in Pennsylvania?
Yes, but only if your rental agreement (or a later change to it) says email is an authorized way to notify you, you affirmatively agreed to be contacted electronically and to keep your email address current, and that consent is printed in bold or underlined in the agreement Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[6].
What is the maximum storage late fee in Pennsylvania?
The law doesn't set a hard cap. It says a late fee of $20 a month or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater, is reasonable and isn't a penalty. The fee and when it's charged must be disclosed in your rental agreement, and no late fee can be charged if you pay rent and other charges in full by the fifth day after the due date Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[4].
Do I get the extra money if my Pennsylvania storage unit sold for more than I owed?
Yes. The owner may pay its lien from the sale and must hold the rest for you on demand. If you don't claim it within six months of the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property and reported and paid to the State Treasurer Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[12], so you can search for it on Pennsylvania's unclaimed property site Pennsylvania Treasury[18].
Can a storage facility tow my car in Pennsylvania?
If you've been in default continuously for at least 60 days and the property is a motor vehicle, trailer or watercraft, the owner may have it towed Pennsylvania General Assembly (official text of Act 51 of 2025)[5].
