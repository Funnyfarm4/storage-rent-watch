How to Stop a Storage Unit Auction in New York (and Claim Leftover Sale Money)

Steps to stop a New York storage lien sale under Lien Law 182: check the 30-day notice, challenge the lien within 10 days, redeem, and claim leftover money.

If your New York storage unit is headed for a lien sale, you usually still have time to act. New York's self-storage rules are in Lien Law section 182, which calls the renter the occupant and the facility the owner Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. Here are the steps, in order, based on the statute text. Read your own occupancy agreement and notice too.

Step 1: Find the key dates on your notice

Before a sale, the facility must send a notice to everyone known to claim an interest in the goods. It must include Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]:

  • an itemized statement of the amount due
  • a description of the property subject to the lien
  • the nature of the proposed sale and its time and place
  • a demand for payment within a stated time of at least 30 days from mailing of the notice
  • a conspicuous statement that if you don't pay in time, the goods will be advertised and sold
  • a statement that you can bring a court proceeding within 10 days of service if you dispute the lien or the amount

Write down two dates right away: the 10-day deadline to go to court, and the payment deadline. New York's general lien-sale article also calls for the sale to be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a local newspaper, with the sale at least 15 days after the first ad, for property worth $100 or more Justia (2025 New York Laws)[2]. Whether and how that applies to a particular self-storage sale is a question for a lawyer, but the date of sale in your notice is the one to beat.

Step 2: Check how the notice reached you

The notice must be personally delivered, sent by registered or certified mail to your last known address, or sent by verified mail plus email Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. Verified mail means a mailing method that gives evidence of mailing, such as first-class mail with a certificate of mailing Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].

Email only counts if your agreement says you consented to late or lien notices by email and you gave your email address in at least two places in it Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. Your last known address is the street, PO box or email address you gave in the agreement or later under it, so keep it current in writing Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. If something about the notice looks wrong, write down what you received and when, and contact free legal help.

Step 3: Check the amount

Compare every charge on the itemized statement with your occupancy agreement. New York requires the agreement to list the actual monthly charge in dollars and an itemized list of every other charge that is or may be imposed, with each amount in dollars and whether it's mandatory or optional Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. The lien covers occupancy fees and other charges, expenses to preserve the property, reasonable costs of selling it, and other charges under the agreement Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. Ask in writing about anything you don't recognize.

Step 4: If the bill is wrong, go to court within 10 days

If you think the facility isn't entitled to a lien, or part of the amount wasn't properly charged, you can bring a special proceeding within 10 days of being served the notice. It can be filed in any court that could enter a judgment for the amount of the lien Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].

  • If the court cancels the lien, you're entitled to your property once the judgment and notice of entry are served on the facility Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
  • If the court finds part of the amount was wrong, it reduces the lien Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
  • If the facility proves the lien, the judgment fixes the amount, and the sale can go ahead 5 days after the judgment and notice of entry are served on you, unless you redeem first Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].

The 10-day window is short, so contact free legal help (below) as soon as the notice arrives.

Step 5: Pay before the sale and get it in writing

Under the general lien-sale article, you can redeem at any time before the sale by paying the amount due on the lien plus whatever legitimate expenses have already been spent serving the notice and advertising the sale. You're then entitled to your property Justia (2025 New York Laws)[3]. Ask for an itemized payoff in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt saying the lien is paid in full and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.

The statute doesn't require the facility to accept a partial payment. Some facilities may agree to a payment plan or a short delay, but that's up to them, so get any deal in writing with the new deadline.

Step 6: If they won't give your things back

Once you pay the fees the law allows, refusing to hand over your goods is an unlawful detention under New York law Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. You can sue for damages and the return of your goods, and a judgment can be up to three times your actual damages plus reasonable attorney fees Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. The New York Attorney General can also ask a court to stop violations of section 182, and a court can impose a civil penalty of up to $1,000 per violation Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. You can file a complaint with the Attorney General New York State Attorney General[5].

After a sale: claim the leftover money

Under the general lien-sale article, the facility keeps enough of the sale money to cover the lien and the costs of advertising and sale. It holds the rest for you and must serve you a notice, personally or by mail, that it's holding the balance Justia (2025 New York Laws)[4]. If you don't claim it within 30 days of the sale, the facility deposits it with the treasurer or chamberlain of the city or village, the Commissioner of Finance in New York City, or the town supervisor where the sale was held. That office pays it to you on demand with satisfactory proof of identity Justia (2025 New York Laws)[4]. After 5 years unclaimed, it goes into that local government's general funds, so don't wait Justia (2025 New York Laws)[4].

Start by asking the facility in writing, and keep a copy. If more than 30 days have passed, ask the facility which office got the deposit; the facility has to file an affidavit with the deposit listing what was sold and the prices Justia (2025 New York Laws)[4].

Frequently asked questions

How much time do I have to stop a storage auction in New York?
The lien notice must demand payment within a stated time of at least 30 days from when it was mailed, and it must give the time and place of the sale Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1]. Under New York's general lien-sale article you can redeem any time before the sale itself Justia (2025 New York Laws)[3]. If you want to dispute the bill in court, the window is much shorter: within 10 days of being served the notice Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
What do I have to pay to stop the sale?
The general lien-sale article says you redeem by paying the amount due on the lien plus whatever legitimate expenses have already been spent serving the notice and advertising the sale Justia (2025 New York Laws)[3]. Ask the facility for an itemized payoff in writing; the notice itself must include an itemized statement of the amount due Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
Can I take the facility to court to stop a storage auction in New York?
Yes. Lien Law 182 lets anyone claiming an interest in the goods bring a special proceeding within 10 days of service of the notice if they dispute the lien or the amount. If the court finds the lien invalid, it cancels it and you're entitled to your property; if it finds part of the amount wasn't properly charged, it reduces the lien. If the lien is upheld, the sale can go ahead 5 days after the judgment and notice of entry are served on you, unless you redeem first Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
Does an emailed lien notice count in New York?
Only if it was sent together with verified mail, your agreement says you consented to late or lien notices by email, and you gave your email address in at least two places in the agreement. An emailed notice is treated as delivered once a non-automated reply or a delivery receipt comes back Justia (2025 New York Laws)[1].
Where does leftover auction money go in New York?
Under the general lien-sale article, the facility keeps enough to cover the lien and the costs of advertising and sale, holds the rest for you, and must notify you. If it isn't claimed within 30 days of the sale, it's deposited with the treasurer or chamberlain of the city or village, the Commissioner of Finance in New York City, or the town supervisor where the sale was held. That office pays it on demand with satisfactory proof of identity. After 5 years unclaimed, it goes into the local government's general funds Justia (2025 New York Laws)[4].
