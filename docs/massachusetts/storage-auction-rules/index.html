Massachusetts Storage Unit Auction Rules (M.G.L. c. 105A): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Massachusetts storage auction rules: lockout after 5 days, two notices, 14 days to pay, ads, late fee limit, stopping the sale and leftover money.

Massachusetts' self-storage law is General Laws Chapter 105A, sections 1 to 9 Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant, and the operator includes the owner, lessor or sublessor, an agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

The operator's lien

The operator has a lien on everything stored in your unit for rent, labor, insurance or other charges, plus expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably incurred in selling it under the law Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[4]. "Default" means failing to do anything the rental agreement requires on time Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2]. Until a lien sale, you keep exclusive care, custody and control of your stored property, unless the law or your rental agreement says otherwise Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[8].

Your rental agreement must tell you, in bold type, that the operator doesn't insure your stored property, that the lien exists, that your property may be sold to satisfy the lien if you're in default, any limit on the value of what you store, and that a late fee may be charged each month rent is late. It must also state the date rent is due and the date the late fee accrues Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[4].

Late fees

The operator may charge a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent when due. The law says a reasonable late fee may be computed as the greater of $20 a month or 20% of the monthly rent Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[6].

How notices are sent

Unless a section says otherwise, notices must go by verified mail or email to your last known address Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[7]. Verified mail is any USPS or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your rental agreement, or in a later written change-of-address notice, so keep both current Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2].

If the operator offers notice by email, your rental agreement must say in bold type that you may agree to get notices by email only, and your choice must be shown in the agreement Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[4]. A mailed notice counts as delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service; an email notice counts as delivered when an electronic delivery receipt is obtained Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[7].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: Default and lockout

If you're in default for 5 days or more, the operator may deny you access to your unit in a reasonable and peaceable manner. You may still go in at any time for the sole purpose of viewing your things to verify what's there Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[6].

Step 2: First notice (no sooner than 5 days after default)

No sooner than 5 days after default, the operator must notify you, and anyone else it knows claims an interest in the goods, by regular mail or email to your last known address Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5].

Step 3: Second notice (no sooner than 14 days after default)

No sooner than 14 days after default, the operator must notify you again by email, verified mail or hand delivery Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. If it emails a notice of a pending sale and gets no response, return receipt or delivery confirmation from that email address, it must send the notice by verified mail to your last known postal address before going ahead Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. The notice must include:

  • a statement that the contents of your unit are subject to the operator's lien
  • a general description of the contents, if the operator knows them
  • the operator's claim: charges due on the date of the notice, any additional charges that will come due before the sale, and when they come due
  • a demand for payment within a stated time not less than 14 days after you receive the notice
  • a statement that unless the claim is paid in time, the contents will be advertised and sold at auction at a specified time and place
  • the name, street address and phone number of the operator or its agent you can contact

Step 4: Advertising the sale

After your payment deadline passes, the sale must be advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation, in a periodical that circulates at least weekly in the county where the facility is, or in any other commercially reasonable manner. The ad must describe the property (if known), name the person whose account it's held on, and give the time and place of the sale. If at least 3 independent bidders attend the sale at the advertised time and place, the advertising is considered commercially reasonable Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5].

Step 5: The sale

The sale may be public or private and must take place at least 15 days after the first publication or posting of the ad. It must be held at the facility, at the nearest suitable place, or on a publicly accessible website, and it must match the terms of the notice. The operator may bid at a public sale Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights even if the operator didn't follow the rules, but the operator is then liable to you for damages, and for a willful violation it's liable for conversion Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5].

Vehicles: if the lien is on a vehicle (including a motorcycle, boat, trailer or RV) and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days after rent was due, the operator may have it towed by an insured tower. Once the tower takes possession, the operator isn't liable for the vehicle Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5] Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2].

How to stop the sale

Before the sale, anyone claiming a right in the property may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the operator incurred under the sale procedure. If you do, the property must not be sold and must be released to you Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The law doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

The operator may pay its lien from the sale money but must hold any balance for release to you on demand (or to anyone it would have had to release the property to) Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. Ask the operator in writing for your balance as soon as you can.

Chapter 105A doesn't say what happens to a balance nobody claims Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[1]. If time has passed, you can also search the Massachusetts State Treasurer's unclaimed property site under your name Massachusetts State Treasurer[11].

Other Massachusetts rules

  • Unfair or deceptive practices: any violation of Chapter 105A by an operator is treated as an unfair or deceptive act or practice under the state consumer protection law, Chapter 93A Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[9].
  • Abandoned units: if a unit is found unlocked and empty (or with property the operator thinks is worth under $300), or you've affirmatively surrendered it, the operator may take it back after 14 days and dispose of what's inside, but only after trying to contact you on 2 separate days at least 3 days apart, or your emergency contact if that fails. This doesn't replace the default and sale rules above Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[10] Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2].
  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement limits the value of what you store, that limit is treated as the maximum value of your property and the most the operator can owe on any claim Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[4].
  • No living in units: you may not use a storage unit as a residence, and the operator may not knowingly allow it Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[3].
  • Warehouses: the law doesn't treat a warehouse operator as an "operator" unless it issues a warehouse receipt, bill of lading or other document of title for the stored property Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[2].
  • Rent increases: the law doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[1]. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Massachusetts?
The law sets minimums, not a fixed date. A first notice can be sent no sooner than 5 days after default and a second notice no sooner than 14 days after default. The second notice must give you at least 14 days after you receive it to pay. After that, the sale is advertised once a week for 2 consecutive weeks and can take place no sooner than 15 days after the first ad Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5].
Can a Massachusetts storage facility send auction notices by email?
Yes, notices may go by email or verified mail Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[7]. But if the operator emails the notice of a pending sale and gets no response, return receipt or delivery confirmation from that email address, it must also send the notice by verified mail to your last known postal address before going ahead with the sale Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. Your rental agreement must tell you in bold type if you can choose to get notices by email only Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[4].
Can a Massachusetts storage facility lock me out?
If you're in default for 5 days or more, the operator may deny you access in a reasonable and peaceable manner. You can still have access at any time for the sole purpose of viewing the contents of your unit to verify what's there Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[6].
How do I stop a storage auction in Massachusetts?
Before the sale, pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien plus the reasonable expenses the operator incurred under the sale procedure. Then the property must not be sold and must be released to you Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
Can they tow my car from a Massachusetts storage unit?
If the lien is on a vehicle and rent and other charges stay unpaid for 60 days after rent was due, the operator may have the vehicle towed by an insured tower. Once the tower takes it, the operator isn't liable for the vehicle Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5].
What happens to the leftover money after a Massachusetts storage auction?
The operator may pay its lien from the sale money but must hold any balance for release to you on demand Massachusetts Legislature (General Court)[5]. Ask for it in writing as soon as you can.
