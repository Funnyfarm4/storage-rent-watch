Tennessee Storage Unit Auction Rules (Tenn. Code §§ 66-31-101 to 66-31-107): Notices, Timeline and Your Rights

Tennessee storage auction rules: lockout, 15-day default, 30-day demand notice, no sale before 60 days, late fee cap, redemption and 1-year leftover money.

Quick answer

  • If you don't pay rent when due, the owner may deny you access without notice, and 5 or more days after rent was due may move your things to other storage pending sale Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
  • To sell, you must be in default for a continuous 15 days, and the written notice must give you at least 30 days from the date of the notice to pay Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
  • The notice must say your property will be sold or disposed of at a specified time and place not sooner than 60 days after default Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
  • You can redeem your property before any sale by paying the amount needed to satisfy the lien plus reasonable expenses Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
  • Late fees can't be more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of monthly rent Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4].

Tennessee's self-storage law is the Self-Service Storage Facility Act, Tenn. Code §§ 66-31-101 to 66-31-107 Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[1]. In this law the renter is the occupant, and the owner includes the operator, lessor, sublessor, their agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on all personal property at the facility for rent, labor, or other charges, plus expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably incurred selling it. The lien attaches as soon as your property goes into the space Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4]. It ranks above other liens except those perfected and recorded in Tennessee in your name during the rental term, and tax liens Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4].

Your rental agreement must state in bold type that the lien exists and how it is enforced, and must state the late fee, if any, and when it applies Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4]. It must also say your property may be sold or disposed of if no payment is received for a continuous 15-day period, and must ask you to disclose any lienholder with an interest in property you store Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[6].

Default means failing to perform on time any obligation in the chapter or your rental agreement Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2].

Late fees and lockouts

The owner may charge a reasonable late fee for each month rent isn't paid when due. A reasonable fee is not more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of monthly rent Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4]. You must get adequate notice first, either through the rental agreement or a notice sent to your last known address Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4].

If you don't pay rent when due, the owner may deny you access without notice. Not less than 5 days after rent was due, it may also enter and move your property to other suitable storage pending sale or other disposition. It must send you written notice of its intent to enforce its lien by hand delivery, verified mail, or email Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

The timeline, step by step

Step 1: 15 days of continuous default

The sale process applies to a long-term default, which the law defines as a continuous 15 days Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

Step 2: The written notice (at least 30 days to pay)

The owner must notify you in writing by hand delivery, verified mail, or email to your last known address Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. Verified mail means any U.S. Postal Service method that gives evidence of mailing. Your last known address is the street address, P.O. box or email address in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep it current Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2].

The notice must include Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]:

  • an itemized statement of the amount due when the notice is sent and when it became due
  • a demand for payment within a time not less than 30 days after the date of the notice, and the approximate added expenses that may build up before the sale
  • a statement that the contents of your space are subject to the owner's lien
  • a statement if the owner is denying you access or moving your property to other storage
  • the name, street address and phone number of the owner or agent you can contact
  • a conspicuous statement that unless the claim is paid in time, your property will be advertised for sale or otherwise disposed of at a specified time and place, not sooner than 60 days after default

The sale must follow the terms of this notice. If a sale is advertised but doesn't happen, the owner must give you written notice of any other disposition Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

Step 3: Advertising

After the notice period runs out, the owner must advertise the sale in a commercially reasonable manner. That standard is met if at least 3 potential bidders take part at the advertised time and place, and the ad may include one notice in a newspaper serving the facility's area Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. The ad must say the contents will be sold to satisfy the lien, give the facility address, the space number or description and your name, and state the time, place and manner of the sale Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

Step 4: The sale

The sale may be held at the facility, online, or at the nearest suitable place to where your property is stored. The owner may bid at the sale Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights, even if the owner didn't follow the rules, and the owner's liability from the sale is limited to the net proceeds Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. But an owner loses the chapter's remedies, including lien enforcement, if it doesn't meet the section's requirements, doesn't advertise properly, or willfully violates the chapter Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

Vehicles and boats: if rent and charges for a vehicle stay unpaid 60 days after rent was due, the owner may have it towed (and isn't liable for it once the tower takes it), or it may look up any lienholder and the registered owner and send them notice by verified mail giving 30 days to pay before selling the vehicle Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. "Vehicle" includes motor vehicles, trailers, semitrailers and vessels Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2].

How to stop the sale

Before any sale or other disposition, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the owner's lien plus the reasonable expenses it incurred enforcing it, and redeem your property. Once you pay, the owner must return your property Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The chapter doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

Leftover money from the sale

After a sale, the owner may pay its lien and sale expenses from the proceeds but must hold the balance for delivery to you on demand. If you don't claim it within 1 year of the sale date, it is deemed abandoned and the owner must pay it to the state treasurer under the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].

Within that year, ask the owner in writing for the balance. After that, search and claim through the Tennessee Treasury's official unclaimed property program Tennessee Department of Treasury[7].

Other Tennessee rules

  • Ending your rental: if the owner delivers written notice that your rental agreement is ending or won't be renewed, you get at least 15 days after delivery to remove everything. The owner may limit access to office hours meanwhile and may dispose of anything left after the date in the notice Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[6].
  • Changes to your agreement: if the owner delivers a written revision and you don't sign it, using the space for 30 days after the revision date counts as accepting it Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[6]. The chapter sets no rent-increase notice period. See My storage rent went up. What can I do?
  • Value caps in the lease: if your rental agreement limits the value of property you may store, that limit is deemed the maximum value of what's in your space Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[4].
  • Owner entry: on reasonable request you must let the owner in for inspection, repairs or services; in an emergency it may enter without notice Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[3].
  • Identity theft: the owner isn't liable for misuse of information in documents or storage media sold with your property if it didn't actually know about them Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. Remove papers and drives with personal information if you can.

Frequently asked questions

How long before a storage unit is auctioned in Tennessee?
Lien enforcement by sale starts once you've been in default for a continuous 15 days. The owner must then send written notice demanding payment within at least 30 days after the date of the notice, and the sale or other disposition can't be set sooner than 60 days after default Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
Can a Tennessee storage facility lock me out?
Yes. If you don't pay rent when due, the owner may deny you access without notice. Not less than 5 days after rent was due, it may also enter and move your property to other suitable storage pending sale or other disposition, and it must send you written notice of its intent to enforce its lien Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5].
Can a Tennessee storage facility send auction notices by email?
Yes. The law allows notice by hand delivery, verified mail, or email to your last known address Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. Your last known address includes an email address you gave in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep it current Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2].
How do I stop a storage auction in Tennessee?
Before any sale or other disposition, pay the amount necessary to satisfy the owner's lien plus the reasonable expenses incurred enforcing it. The owner must then return your property Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. Get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled.
What happens to leftover money after a Tennessee storage auction?
The owner may pay its lien and sale expenses from the proceeds but must hold the balance for delivery to you on demand. If you don't claim it within 1 year of the sale, it is deemed abandoned and paid to the state treasurer under the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5]. You can then search Tennessee's official unclaimed property site Tennessee Department of Treasury[7].
Can they tow my car or boat from a Tennessee storage unit?
If rent and other charges on a vehicle (including a boat) stay unpaid for 60 days after rent was due, the owner may have it towed, or it may look up any lienholder and the registered owner and give them 30 days' notice to pay before selling it Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[5] Justia (full text of the Tennessee Code)[2].
