California's self-storage law is the **California Self-Service Storage Facility Act**, Business and Professions Code sections 21700 to 21716 [[ca-ch]]. In it, the renter is the **occupant** and the facility is the **owner** [[ca-01]]. Below is a plain-English summary of what the statute says about rent and term changes. Your rental agreement can add terms [[ca-13]], so read it alongside this page.

## The 30-day notice rule

The owner may change the terms of your rental agreement, **including rent**, after giving you **at least 30 days' written notice** [[ca-155]]. Notice must be given by one of these methods [[ca-155]]:

- personal delivery
- first-class mail, postage prepaid, to your last known address
- email, but only under the email rules in section 21712 (the agreement must allow email notices and you must have signed consenting) [[ca-12]]

Once at least 30 days have passed from the date of service, the notice itself establishes the new terms, rents and conditions **if you keep storing** personal property at the facility after it takes effect [[ca-155]]. Leaving before then is how you avoid the new rent.

The chapter does **not** set a dollar or percentage cap on increases, and it does not limit how often rent may change [[ca-ch]] [[ca-155]]. Those details, if any, come from your agreement [[ca-13]].

## New first-page disclosures (agreements on or after January 1, 2026)

For a rental agreement **initially entered into on or after January 1, 2026**, the owner must put clear disclosures on the **first page**, in larger type than the surrounding text and set off by contrast or marks [[ca-152]]. Those disclosures must cover [[ca-152]]:

- the initial length and renewal term
- whether you received a promotional or discounted rental fee, and if so how long it lasts
- whether the rental fee can change and, if so, the **maximum rental fee** the owner could charge during the **first 12 months** after the agreement date
- every required step to terminate and avoid future fees or charges, including removing all personal property from the space
- the owner's contact information

If you are shopping for a unit, ask for those numbers in writing before you sign, even when the agreement is older than that date.

## Why the price went up: teaser rates

Many facilities advertise a low first month or first few months. Once that promotional rate ends, the rent can rise under the notice rule above. Before you sign anywhere, ask two questions and get the answers in writing:

- What will my monthly rent be after any special or introductory rate ends?
- How often can the rent change, and how much notice will I get?

For agreements signed on or after January 1, 2026, the first-page disclosures above should answer both [[ca-152]].

## Keep paying while you sort it out

Don't withhold rent to protest an increase. Unpaid charges can start the lien process after **14 consecutive days**, and the facility can later deny access and sell your property [[ca-03]]. Late fees also apply once rent is at least **10 days** overdue: only one fee per missed payment, the amount must be in your agreement, and the cap is $10 if monthly rent is $60 or less, $15 if rent is over $60 but under $100, or the greater of $20 or 15% of monthly rent if rent is $100 or more [[ca-135]].

For the full lien-sale timeline, declaration in opposition and leftover-money rules, see [California storage auction rules](/california/storage-auction-rules/).

## Negotiate, shrink or move

Ask the manager, politely and in writing, to lower or delay the increase. It helps to have:

- quotes from two or three nearby facilities for the same size unit
- your payment history (on time every month is your best argument)
- a clear ask, such as keeping your current rate for a few more months

If the facility says no, ask whether a smaller unit at the same location would cost less. You can also:

- **Downsize.** Clear out what you don't need and move to a smaller unit.
- **Move to a cheaper facility.** Add up moving costs (truck, time, a new lock, any admin fee) against what you'll save each month.
- **Try peer-to-peer storage.** Neighbor is a marketplace where hosts rent out space such as garages, basements and driveways [[neighbor]]. Compare real quotes and read the host's terms.

## If you think the facility broke the rules

If a facility raised rent without the required 30-day written notice, skipped first-page disclosures on a new agreement, or charged late fees your agreement doesn't allow, you can file a consumer complaint with the [California Attorney General](https://oag.ca.gov/contact/consumer-complaint-against-business-or-company) [[ca-ag]]. If your unit is headed to a lien sale, get legal help now.
