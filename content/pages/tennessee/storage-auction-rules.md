Tennessee's self-storage law is the **Self-Service Storage Facility Act, Tenn. Code §§ 66-31-101 to 66-31-107** [[tn-ch]]. In this law the renter is the **occupant**, and the **owner** includes the operator, lessor, sublessor, their agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent [[tn-102]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

## The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on **all personal property** at the facility for rent, labor, or other charges, plus expenses needed to preserve the property or reasonably incurred selling it. The lien attaches as soon as your property goes into the space [[tn-104]]. It ranks above other liens except those perfected and recorded in Tennessee in your name during the rental term, and tax liens [[tn-104]].

Your rental agreement must state **in bold type** that the lien exists and how it is enforced, and must state the late fee, if any, and when it applies [[tn-104]]. It must also say your property may be sold or disposed of if no payment is received for a **continuous 15-day period**, and must ask you to disclose any lienholder with an interest in property you store [[tn-106]].

**Default** means failing to perform on time any obligation in the chapter or your rental agreement [[tn-102]].

## Late fees and lockouts

The owner may charge a reasonable late fee for each month rent isn't paid when due. A reasonable fee is **not more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of monthly rent** [[tn-104]]. You must get adequate notice first, either through the rental agreement or a notice sent to your last known address [[tn-104]].

If you don't pay rent when due, the owner may **deny you access without notice**. Not less than **5 days** after rent was due, it may also enter and **move your property to other suitable storage** pending sale or other disposition. It must send you written notice of its intent to enforce its lien by hand delivery, verified mail, or email [[tn-105]].

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: 15 days of continuous default

The sale process applies to a **long-term default**, which the law defines as a **continuous 15 days** [[tn-105]].

### Step 2: The written notice (at least 30 days to pay)

The owner must notify you **in writing** by **hand delivery, verified mail, or email** to your last known address [[tn-105]]. Verified mail means any U.S. Postal Service method that gives evidence of mailing. Your last known address is the street address, P.O. box or email address in your latest rental agreement or a later written change-of-address notice, so keep it current [[tn-102]].

The notice must include [[tn-105]]:

- an itemized statement of the amount due when the notice is sent and when it became due
- a demand for payment within a time **not less than 30 days after the date of the notice**, and the approximate added expenses that may build up before the sale
- a statement that the contents of your space are subject to the owner's lien
- a statement if the owner is denying you access or moving your property to other storage
- the name, street address and phone number of the owner or agent you can contact
- a **conspicuous statement** that unless the claim is paid in time, your property will be advertised for sale or otherwise disposed of at a specified time and place, **not sooner than 60 days after default**

The sale must follow the terms of this notice. If a sale is advertised but doesn't happen, the owner must give you written notice of any other disposition [[tn-105]].

### Step 3: Advertising

After the notice period runs out, the owner must advertise the sale in a **commercially reasonable manner**. That standard is met if **at least 3 potential bidders** take part at the advertised time and place, and the ad may include one notice in a newspaper serving the facility's area [[tn-105]]. The ad must say the contents will be sold to satisfy the lien, give the facility address, the space number or description and your name, and state the time, place and manner of the sale [[tn-105]].

### Step 4: The sale

The sale may be held **at the facility, online, or at the nearest suitable place** to where your property is stored. The owner may bid at the sale [[tn-105]]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights, even if the owner didn't follow the rules, and the owner's liability from the sale is limited to the net proceeds [[tn-105]]. But an owner loses the chapter's remedies, including lien enforcement, if it doesn't meet the section's requirements, doesn't advertise properly, or willfully violates the chapter [[tn-105]].

> **Vehicles and boats:** if rent and charges for a vehicle stay unpaid **60 days** after rent was due, the owner may have it **towed** (and isn't liable for it once the tower takes it), or it may look up any lienholder and the registered owner and send them notice by verified mail giving **30 days** to pay before selling the vehicle [[tn-105]]. "Vehicle" includes motor vehicles, trailers, semitrailers and vessels [[tn-102]].

## How to stop the sale

**Before any sale or other disposition**, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the owner's lien **plus the reasonable expenses** it incurred enforcing it, and redeem your property. Once you pay, the owner must return your property [[tn-105]]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The chapter doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice [[tn-105]]. ## Leftover money from the sale

After a sale, the owner may pay its lien and sale expenses from the proceeds but must **hold the balance for delivery to you on demand**. If you don't claim it **within 1 year** of the sale date, it is deemed abandoned and the owner must pay it to the **state treasurer** under the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act [[tn-105]].

Within that year, ask the owner in writing for the balance. After that, search and claim through the Tennessee Treasury's official [unclaimed property program](https://treasury.tn.gov/Unclaimed-Property/Claim-Unclaimed-Property/Find-Your-Missing-Money) [[tn-up]].

## Other Tennessee rules

- **Ending your rental:** if the owner delivers written notice that your rental agreement is ending or won't be renewed, you get **at least 15 days** after delivery to remove everything. The owner may limit access to office hours meanwhile and may dispose of anything left after the date in the notice [[tn-106]].
- **Changes to your agreement:** if the owner delivers a written revision and you don't sign it, using the space for **30 days** after the revision date counts as accepting it [[tn-106]]. The chapter sets no rent-increase notice period. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)
- **Value caps in the lease:** if your rental agreement limits the value of property you may store, that limit is deemed the maximum value of what's in your space [[tn-104]].
- **Owner entry:** on reasonable request you must let the owner in for inspection, repairs or services; in an emergency it may enter without notice [[tn-103]].
- **Identity theft:** the owner isn't liable for misuse of information in documents or storage media sold with your property if it didn't actually know about them [[tn-105]]. Remove papers and drives with personal information if you can.
