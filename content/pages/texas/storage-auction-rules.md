Texas's self-storage lien law is Chapter 59 of the Texas Property Code, "Self-Service Storage Facility Liens" [[tx-ch]]. In it, the renter is called the **tenant** and the facility owner or manager is the **lessor** [[tx-001]]. Below is a plain-English summary of the statute text. Your rental agreement matters too, but it can't waive the rights this chapter gives you unless the chapter itself allows it [[tx-004]].

## The facility's lien

The facility has a lien on all property in your unit for charges that are due and unpaid [[tx-021]]. The lien attaches the day you put your property in the unit and takes priority over other liens on the same property [[tx-006]].

## Court order or contract lien

A facility can normally enforce its lien only with a court judgment that forecloses the lien and orders a sale. It can seize and sell your things **without going to court** only if [[tx-041]]:

- the seizure and sale are under a contractual landlord's lien that is **underlined or printed in conspicuous bold** in a written rental agreement, and
- the seizure and sale follow chapter 59.

Check your agreement for that bold or underlined lien clause.

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Written notice of the claim

The facility must give you written notice of its claim [[tx-042]]. The notice must contain [[tx-043]]:

- an itemized account of the claim
- the name, address and phone number of the lessor or its agent
- a statement that the contents of your unit have been seized under the contractual landlord's lien
- a statement that if you don't pay on or before the **14th day** after the notice is delivered, the property may be sold at public auction (or, for vehicles and boats, towed to a vehicle storage facility)
- an underlined or bold statement asking a tenant in military service to tell the facility about that service right away

The notice must be delivered in person, or by email or verified mail to the email or postal address in your rental agreement or in a later written notice you gave the facility. Verified mail means any mailing method that provides evidence of mailing [[tx-001]]. Mailed notice counts as delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service or a carrier; email counts as delivered when sent [[tx-043]]. **Email is allowed only** if your written agreement says, underlined or in bold, that notice may be given by email if you choose to provide an email address [[tx-043]]. Keep your contact information current with the facility.

### Step 2: Advertising the sale

If you don't pay by the 14th day after delivery, the facility must advertise the sale [[tx-042]]. The notice must be published once in each of **two consecutive weeks** in a newspaper of general circulation in the county where the facility is. Only if there's no such newspaper may the facility instead post the notice at the facility and at least five other conspicuous places nearby [[tx-044]]. The ad must include [[tx-044]]:

- a general description of the property
- a statement that it is being sold to satisfy a landlord's lien
- your name
- the facility's address
- the time, place and terms of the sale (for an online sale, the place is the website address)

### Step 3: The sale

With newspaper notice, the facility may not sell until the **15th day after the first publication**. With posted notice, it may sell after the **10th day** after posting [[tx-042]]. The sale must be public, held at the facility, at a reasonably near public place, or on a website open to the public. It must follow the terms in the ad, and the property goes to the highest bidder [[tx-045]]. A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your claim even if the facility didn't follow chapter 59 [[tx-007]], so act **before** the sale.

> Vehicles and boats: the facility must send a separate notice by verified mail to the last known owner and each recorded lienholder within 30 days of taking possession, and can't sell before the 31st day after that notice is mailed or published [[tx-0445]]. If your rental agreement allows it, the facility may instead have a titled vehicle or boat towed to a vehicle storage facility after the 14-day notice period [[tx-052]].

## How to stop the sale

You can **redeem** your property before it's sold or otherwise disposed of by paying the facility the amount of the lien plus its reasonable expenses under chapter 59 [[tx-008]]. Ask for an itemized payoff amount in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help right away.

If you're a member of the Texas State Guard or Texas National Guard in military service, you get the same storage-lien protections as servicemembers under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act [[tx-010]]. Tell the facility in writing; it may ask for documentation [[tx-043]].

## Leftover money from the sale

If the sale brings in more than the lien and the reasonable expenses of the sale, the facility must send written notice of the excess to your last known address. It holds the excess and must give it to you if you ask **within two years after the sale**. After two years, the facility owns it [[tx-046]]. Unlike some states, Texas's storage chapter doesn't send this money to a state unclaimed property office, so ask the facility directly and in writing.

## If the facility broke the rules

A person injured by a violation of chapter 59 may sue for damages under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act [[tx-005]]. You can also file a consumer complaint with the Texas Attorney General [[tx-ag]].

## Other Texas rules to know

- **No living in the unit:** a tenant may not use a storage unit as a residence or let anyone else do so [[tx-009]].
- **Late fees and rent increases:** chapter 59 has no section setting a late-fee cap or a rent-increase notice period, so your rental agreement controls those terms [[tx-ch]]. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)
