If your Texas storage unit is behind and you've gotten a notice that your things were seized under a landlord's lien, you can still stop the sale. Texas's storage law, Chapter 59 of the Property Code [[tx-ch]], lets you **redeem** your property by paying before it's sold [[tx-008]]. Your rental agreement can't take that right away unless the chapter itself allows it [[tx-004]]. Here's how to use it.

## Step 1: Find the key dates on your notice

The facility must give you a written notice of its claim [[tx-042]]. It must include an itemized account of what you owe, the name, address and phone number of the facility or its agent, a statement that your unit's contents have been seized under the contractual landlord's lien, and a statement that if you don't pay on or before the **14th day** after the notice is delivered, the property may be sold at public auction (or, for vehicles and boats, towed to a vehicle storage facility) [[tx-043]].

Count from the delivery date. Mailed notice counts as delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service or a carrier, and email counts as delivered when sent [[tx-043]]. If you miss the 14-day deadline, the facility must advertise the sale once in each of two consecutive weeks in a county newspaper, or by posting only if the county has no such newspaper [[tx-044]]. With newspaper ads, the sale can't happen until the **15th day after the first ad**; with posted notices, it can happen after the **10th day** [[tx-042]]. Look for the ad, which must list the time, place and terms of the sale (for an online sale, the website address) [[tx-044]].

## Step 2: Check that the facility can sell without a court

A Texas facility can seize and sell your things without a court judgment only if your **written** rental agreement has a contractual landlord's lien that is **underlined or printed in conspicuous bold**, and it follows chapter 59 [[tx-041]]. Also check how the notice reached you: email notice is allowed only if your written agreement says, underlined or in bold, that notice may be given by email if you choose to provide an email address [[tx-043]].

## Step 3: Check the amount

Ask for the itemized account in writing if it isn't on your notice [[tx-043]]. To redeem, you pay the amount of the lien plus the facility's **reasonable** expenses under chapter 59 [[tx-008]]. Chapter 59 doesn't set a late-fee cap, so the late fees come from your rental agreement [[tx-ch]]. Compare each charge with your agreement and ask about anything you don't recognize.

## Step 4: Pay before the sale and get it in writing

Before the sale or other disposition, pay the facility the lien amount plus its reasonable expenses to redeem your property [[tx-008]]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, get a receipt that says the lien is paid in full, and ask for written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. Don't wait for the auction day: a good-faith buyer takes the property free of your claim **even if the facility didn't follow chapter 59** [[tx-007]].

## Step 5: If you can't pay it all

The redemption right in chapter 59 is for the full lien amount plus reasonable expenses [[tx-008]]. Some facilities may agree to a payment plan or a short delay, but that's up to them. If you reach a deal, get it in writing with the new deadline. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) the same day.

## Step 6: Tell them if you're in the military

The notice must ask, underlined or in bold, that a tenant in military service tell the facility right away, and the facility may ask for proof such as Department of Defense documentation [[tx-043]]. Members of the Texas State Guard or Texas National Guard in military service get the same storage-lien protections as servicemembers under the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act [[tx-010]]. Military service for Guard members means active duty for more than 30 days in a row under the governor's call [[tx-001]].

## Cars, trucks and boats

For a titled vehicle or boat, the facility must also send written notice by verified mail to the last known owner and each recorded lienholder within 30 days after taking possession. The owner or lienholder can take it back by paying all charges before the **31st day** after that notice is mailed or published [[tx-0445]]. If your rental agreement allows it, the facility may instead have the vehicle or boat towed to a vehicle storage facility once the 14-day notice period runs out [[tx-052]].

## After a sale: claim the leftover money

If the sale brought in more than the lien and the reasonable expenses of the sale, the facility must send written notice of the extra to your last known address. It holds that money and must pay it to you if you ask **before two years after the sale**. After that, the facility owns it [[tx-046]]. Unlike some states, Texas's storage chapter doesn't send this money to a state unclaimed property office, so ask the facility directly, in writing, and keep a copy.

## If the facility broke the rules

A person injured by a violation of chapter 59 may sue for damages under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act [[tx-005]]. You can also file a consumer complaint with the Texas Attorney General [[tx-ag]].
