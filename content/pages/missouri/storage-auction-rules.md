Missouri's self-storage law is the **Self-Service Storage Facilities Act, RSMo 415.400 to 415.425** [[mo-400]] [[mo-ch]]. In this law the renter is the **occupant** (a person, lessee, sublessee, successor or assignee entitled to use the space), and the **operator** is the owner, operator, lessor or sublessor, or an agent or other person authorized to manage the facility [[mo-405]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text, cited by section; your rental agreement matters too.

## The operator's lien

The operator has a **lien on all personal property stored in your space** for rent, labor or other charges, and for expenses reasonably incurred in selling the property. It has priority over all other liens except liens that were perfected and recorded on the property [[mo-415]] (subsection 1).

Your **rental agreement** must contain a statement **in bold type** telling you about the lien, that your property may be sold to satisfy it if you default, and that leftover sale money will go to the **state treasurer** if you don't claim it within **one year** after the sale [[mo-415]] (subsection 1).

Before you move anything in, you must give the operator (or list in the agreement) the **name and address of anyone with a valid lien** on your property and of any **third-party owner** of what you store, with a description of that property. The operator must also tell you **in writing** whether or not it has **casualty insurance** on your property. If the agreement **limits the value** of what you may store, that limit is treated as the maximum value of your property and the most the owner owes for loss or damage [[mo-410]].

You're in **default** when you fail to perform **on time** any obligation or duty in the rental agreement [[mo-405]]. Until a lien sale, and unless your agreement says otherwise, you keep **exclusive care, custody and control** of what you store [[mo-425]].

## Late fees

A late fee must be **stated in your rental agreement** (or an addendum); otherwise it can't be collected. The operator may charge a **reasonable late fee for each month** you don't pay rent when due. A late fee of **$20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater**, for each late payment is deemed reasonable and not a penalty. The operator can set a higher fee only if it's reasonable, and it **has the burden of proving** that [[mo-417]].

On top of late fees, the operator may recover **all reasonable rent collection and lien enforcement expenses** from you [[mo-417]] (subsection 6). Ask for an itemized list of any such charges.

## Lockouts

If you're in default, the operator **may deny you access** to your leased space [[mo-420]] (subsection 3). The operator may also enter the space whenever reasonably necessary to protect and preserve the facility or the property stored there [[mo-410]] (subsection 2).

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Default

Default starts as soon as you miss an obligation in your agreement, such as rent, on time [[mo-405]]. The operator may lock you out from then on [[mo-420]].

### Step 2: First notice (at least 45 days before the sale)

At least **45 days before** any sale, the operator must notify you of the default by **first-class mail or email** at your last known address. It must also notify each lienholder you listed and any third-party owner you identified. This 45-day notice period runs **at the same time as** the 45-day default period, not after it [[mo-415]] (subsection 4).

### Step 3: Second notice (at least 10 days to pay)

**No sooner than 10 days after** mailing the first notice, the operator must send a **second notice by verified mail** (any Postal Service or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing) **or email** [[mo-405]] [[mo-415]]. It must include [[mo-415]] (subsection 4):

- a statement that the contents of your space are **subject to the operator's lien**
- the operator's **claim**: the charges due on the date of the notice, any additional charges that will come due before the date of release for sale, and when they come due
- a **demand for payment within a set time, not less than 10 days after** the second notice was mailed
- a statement that unless you pay in time, the contents **will be sold** after a specified time
- the **name, street address and phone number** of the operator or a designated agent you can contact

> **How notices count:** a mailed notice is deemed delivered when it's deposited with the Postal Service, properly addressed and stamped; an emailed notice counts when it's sent to your last known email address [[mo-420]] (subsection 4). Your last known address is the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or in a later **written** change-of-address notice, and you can designate one of them in writing as your preferred contact method, which the operator must use [[mo-405]]. Keep both current with the facility in writing.

### Step 4: Advertising the sale (at least 7 days before)

At least **7 days before the sale**, the operator must advertise the **time, place and terms** of the sale in the **classified section** of a newspaper of general circulation where the sale will be held. The ad must say the items will be released for sale [[mo-415]] (subsection 4).

### Step 5: The sale (after more than 45 days in default)

The operator can sell only if you've been in default for **more than 45 days**. The sale must be **for cash** and may be public or private, as one unit or in parcels, at any time or place and on any terms, as long as it's done in a **commercially reasonable manner** [[mo-415]] (subsection 2). A public sale can be held at the facility or **online on a publicly accessible website** [[mo-405]]. Under the commercial-reasonableness rules the act points to, a sale is generally reasonable if it's made in the usual way on a recognized market, at the current market price, or in line with reasonable practices among dealers in that kind of property, and the fact that a higher price was possible doesn't by itself make it unreasonable [[mo-9627]].

The operator may otherwise dispose of property that has **no commercial value**, which includes property offered for sale in a commercially reasonable way that gets **no bid or offer** [[mo-415]] [[mo-405]].

> **Vehicles, boats and trailers:** if the property is a vehicle, watercraft or trailer and rent and other charges stay unpaid for **60 days**, the owner may treat it as an **abandoned vehicle** and have it **towed**. Once the tow company takes possession, the facility owner isn't liable for any damage to it [[mo-415]] (subsection 5).

## How to stop the sale

**At any time before the sale**, you may pay **the amount needed to satisfy the lien** and redeem your property [[mo-415]] (subsection 6). Ask for the exact payoff amount in writing (rent, late fees and any collection or enforcement expenses), pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The law doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

A buyer in good faith takes your property **free and clear** of your rights and other liens, so act **before** the sale [[mo-420]] (subsection 1).

## Leftover money from the sale

Sale money first pays the lien. Any surplus is **held for 1 year** after the sale for delivery **on demand** to you or to other lienholders the operator knows about or you listed [[mo-415]] (subsection 3).

To be paid, you must give the operator a **sworn affidavit** stating that there are no other valid liens on the property sold and that you'll **indemnify the operator** for damages or money it pays because of claims from other lienholders [[mo-415]] (subsection 3). Ask the facility in writing for your balance as soon as you can, and keep a copy.

After the 1-year period, the leftover money is treated as **abandoned property** and is reported and paid to the **Missouri State Treasurer** under the unclaimed property law [[mo-415]] (subsection 3). The Treasurer's Office lets you search for unclaimed property and track a claim online, and says it never charges to return unclaimed property [[mo-up]].

## If the operator breaks the rules

- **Liability limit depends on following the law:** the operator's liability to you is limited to the **net proceeds** of the sale only **if it complied** with the act [[mo-420]] (subsection 2). If it didn't follow the notice and sale rules, talk to a lawyer about your options.
- **Complain to the state:** you can file a consumer complaint with the **Missouri Attorney General's Office**, which enforces the Merchandising Practices Act and mediates complaints between consumers and businesses [[mo-ago]].
- The act itself doesn't spell out a separate penalty or a specific lawsuit right for violations [[mo-ch]], which is another reason to get free legal help early.

## Other Missouri rules

- **No living in units:** an occupant may not use a leased space for residential purposes, and the operator may not knowingly allow it [[mo-410]] (subsection 1).
- **Rent increases:** the act doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls [[mo-ch]]. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)
