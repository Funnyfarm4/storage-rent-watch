Maryland's self-storage law is in the **Commercial Law Article, §§ 18-501 to 18-506** [[md-501]]. In this law the renter is the **occupant**, and the **operator** includes the owner, operator, lessor, sublessor, agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility [[md-501]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too.

## The operator's lien

The operator has a lien on **all personal property** stored in your space for rent, labor, or other charges, and for expenses reasonably incurred in selling it [[md-503]]. Your rental agreement must say in **bold type** that the lien exists, that your property may be sold if you're in default, that a motor vehicle or watercraft may be towed or removed if you're in default for more than 60 days, and that a lien sale will be advertised in a newspaper, by email, or on an online website [[md-503]].

**Default** means failing to perform on time any obligation in the rental agreement [[md-501]]. Until a lien sale, and unless the agreement says otherwise, care, custody and control of your stored property stays with you [[md-505]].

## Late fees and lockouts

The operator may charge a reasonable late fee for each month rent isn't paid on time, but it **may not be more than the greater of $20 a month or 20% of the monthly rent**. It can only be charged if the rental agreement discloses the **amount** of the fee and **when** it is charged [[md-504]].

If you're in default, the operator **may deny you access** to your space [[md-504]].

The subtitle does **not** set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement [[md-501]]. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Notice of default

Before any sale, the operator must notify you of the default by **hand delivery, verified mail, or email** at your last known address [[md-504]]. Verified mail means any USPS or private delivery method that provides evidence of mailing [[md-501]]. Your last known address is the postal or email address in your rental agreement, or one you later gave in a written change-of-address notice, so keep it current [[md-501]].

The notice must include [[md-504]]:

- a statement that the contents of your space are subject to the operator's lien
- the operator's claim: charges due on the date of the notice, any additional charges that will come due before the sale, and when they come due
- a demand for payment within a specified time, **not less than 14 days after the notice was mailed**
- a statement that unless the claim is paid in time, your contents will be sold at a specified time and place
- the name, street address and phone number of the operator or its agent you can contact

**Email** is allowed only if the rental agreement (or a written change to it) says in **bold type** that notice may be given by email **and** you initialed next to that statement. If the operator emails the default notice and gets no reply from your email address or delivery confirmation, it must send a **second notice by verified mail** to your last known postal address [[md-504]].

### Step 2: Notice of sale (at least 10 days before)

At least **10 days before** the sale, the operator must tell you the **time, place, and terms** of the sale by hand delivery, verified mail, or email. If an emailed sale notice gets no reply or delivery confirmation at least 5 days before the sale, the operator must promptly send a second notice by verified mail [[md-504]].

### Step 3: Advertising (at least 3 days before)

At least **3 days before** the sale, the operator must advertise its time, place and terms in **a newspaper of general circulation** where the sale will be held, **by email**, or **on an online website** [[md-504]].

### Step 4: The sale (after more than 60 days in default)

The operator may sell only if you've been in default for **more than 60 days**, and only at a **public sale, for cash** [[md-504]]. The sale must be held at the facility where your property is stored; a sale on an **online auction website** counts as held at the facility [[md-504]].

A good-faith buyer takes the property free of your rights and other lienholders' rights. If the operator follows the subtitle, its liability to you is limited to the net sale proceeds [[md-504]].

> **Vehicles and boats:** if you've been in default **more than 60 days** and the stored property is a motor vehicle or watercraft, the operator may have it **towed or removed** instead of selling it. The operator isn't liable for damage after the towing company takes possession [[md-504]].

## How to stop the sale

**At any time before the sale**, you may pay the amount necessary to satisfy the lien and redeem your property [[md-504]]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The subtitle doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, call the contact listed in your notice of default [[md-504]]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

## Leftover money from the sale

After a sale, the operator must pay the lien from the proceeds and **mail any balance by certified mail** to you or any other recorded lienholder at the last known address [[md-504]]. If that mail comes back, the operator must **hold the balance for 1 year** after the sale for delivery on demand. After that year, the balance is **presumed abandoned** [[md-504]] [[md-1730]].

Unclaimed property in Maryland is handled by the Comptroller; search and claim at the official [MD Unclaimed Property](https://www.claimitmd.gov/) site [[md-up]]. Within that first year, contact the operator directly in writing.

## Other Maryland rules

- **No living in units:** the operator may not knowingly allow a space to be used as a residence, and you may not use it as one [[md-502]].
- **Value caps in the lease:** if your rental agreement limits the value of property you may store, that limit is deemed the maximum value of your stored property [[md-504]].
- **Notices to the operator** must go to the facility where your property is stored, by hand delivery or verified mail [[md-504]].
