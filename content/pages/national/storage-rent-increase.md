A storage unit that started cheap can cost a lot more a year later. Here is what to check, what the law says where it says anything, and how to decide whether to negotiate or move.

## Why the price went up: teaser rates

If your first month, or first few months, were at a special or introductory price, your rent can rise once that price ends, and moving your things out is a hassle. Before you sign anywhere, ask two questions and get the answers in writing:

- What will my monthly rent be after any special or introductory rate ends?
- How often can the rent change, and how much notice will I get?

## Step 1: Read your rental agreement

State storage laws mostly cover the facility's lien, late fees and lien sales, and leave the rent itself to your contract. Indiana's storage law, for example, says it doesn't limit the parties' power to create their own rights and duties in the rental agreement [[in-16]]. Look for:

- the **term** (month to month or a fixed period)
- any clause on **changing rent or terms**, and the notice it promises
- **late fees** and when they kick in
- **value limits** on what you store, and insurance requirements

## Step 2: Check your state's notice rule

Some states set a minimum notice period for changing a storage rental agreement:

- **California:** the owner may change the terms, including rent, only after **at least 30 days' written notice** by personal delivery, first-class mail to your last known address, or agreed email. The new terms take effect after 30 days if you keep storing there [[ca-21715]]. See [California storage auction rules](/california/storage-auction-rules/) for its late-fee caps and new first-page rent disclosures.
- **Florida**, **Indiana**, **New York**, **Ohio** and **Texas:** their storage laws don't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls [[fl-ch]] [[in-ch]] [[ny-182]] [[oh-ch]] [[tx-ch]]. New York does require your agreement to state the actual monthly charge and every other charge in dollars [[ny-182]]. See [Florida storage auction rules](/florida/storage-auction-rules/), [New York storage auction rules](/new-york/storage-auction-rules/) and [Texas storage auction rules](/texas/storage-auction-rules/).

## Step 3: Keep paying while you sort it out

Don't withhold rent to protest. Unpaid rent is a default, and it costs money fast:

- **Late fees:** Indiana allows up to the greater of $20 or 20% of monthly rent for each month in default [[in-115]]. Ohio allows a reasonable late fee stated in the agreement, but none if you pay in full by the third day after the due date [[oh-05]]. Florida treats a late fee of $20 or 20% of monthly rent, whichever is greater, as reasonable, if the fee is stated in your agreement [[fl-808]].
- **Lockout and sale:** in Indiana, a facility can start enforcing its lien after 5 days of continuous default [[in-12]]. In Ohio, the lien notice can demand payment in as little as 10 days [[oh-03]].

## Step 4: Negotiate

Ask the manager, politely and in writing, to lower or delay the increase. It helps to have:

- quotes from two or three nearby facilities for the same size unit
- your payment history (on time every month is your best argument)
- a clear ask, such as keeping your current rate for six months

If the facility says no, ask whether a smaller unit at the same location would cost less.

## Step 5: Shrink or move

- **Downsize.** Clear out what you don't need and move to a smaller unit.
- **Move to a cheaper facility.** Add up moving costs (truck, time, a new lock, any admin fee) against what you'll save each month.
- **Try peer-to-peer storage.** Neighbor is a marketplace where hosts rent out space such as garages, basements and driveways [[neighbor]]. It advertises lower prices than traditional storage [[neighbor]]; compare real quotes and read the host's terms.

## If you think the facility broke the rules

If a facility charged fees your agreement doesn't allow, or ignored a notice rule, you can complain to your state attorney general's consumer protection office, for example in [Indiana](https://www.in.gov/attorneygeneral/consumer-protection-division/) [[in-ag]] or [Ohio](https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Individuals-and-Families/Consumers) [[oh-ag]]. If your unit is headed to a lien sale, get legal help now.
