If your Indiana storage unit is behind on rent and you've received a lien notice, you can still stop the sale. Indiana's storage law, IC 26-3-8 [[in-ch]], gives you a right to **redeem** your property by paying before the sale [[in-13]]. Here's how to use it.

## Step 1: Find the key dates on your notice

Your lien notice must list what you owe, the date it became due, a payment deadline at least 30 days after the notice was mailed, a contact name, street address and phone number, and the place and time of the planned sale, which must be at least 60 days after your default [[in-12]]. Write down the payment deadline and the sale date. The sale itself must be held at least 10 days after it's advertised [[in-14]].

## Step 2: Check the amount

Ask for the itemized statement in writing if you don't have it. The lien can include rent, late fees, rent collection costs, preservation expenses and reasonable sale or lien enforcement expenses [[in-11]]. Check the late fees: for each month in default, Indiana caps the late fee at the greater of **$20** or **20% of the monthly rent** [[in-115]].

## Step 3: Pay before the sale and get a receipt

Before any sale or other disposal, you can redeem your property by paying the owner an amount sufficient to satisfy the lien. The owner must then return your property immediately [[in-13]]. Pay in a way that leaves a record, get a written receipt that says the lien is paid in full, and ask the facility to confirm in writing that the sale is cancelled.

## Step 4: If you can't pay it all

Indiana's redemption right is for the full lien amount [[in-13]]. Some facilities may agree to a payment plan or a short delay, but that's up to them. If you reach a deal, get it in writing, with the new deadline. If the sale date is close and you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) the same day.

## Step 5: Make sure your address is up to date

Notices go to your **last known address**, meaning the postal or email address in your latest rental agreement or in a written change-of-address notice you gave the facility [[in-3]]. If you've moved or changed email, tell the facility in writing.

{{legal-help}}

## After a sale: claim the leftover money

If your unit was already sold, the facility can keep what it's owed from the sale. Any extra must be held for you and delivered when you ask. If you don't claim it within one year after the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property under Indiana law [[in-15]]. Search for it on Indiana's official unclaimed property site, run by the Attorney General [[in-unclaimed]].

To complain about a storage facility, you can contact the Indiana Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division [[in-ag]].
