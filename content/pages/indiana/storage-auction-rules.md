Indiana's self-storage law is Indiana Code 26-3-8, "Self-Service Storage Facilities" [[in-ch]]. The official text is published by the Indiana General Assembly [[iga]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of that chapter as published in the 2025 Indiana Code. Your rental agreement can add terms of its own, so read it too.

## What counts as default

"Default" means not doing, on time, something the rental agreement requires, such as paying rent [[in-1]]. The facility's lien attaches the day you put property in the unit [[in-11]], and every Indiana storage rental agreement must include a **bold** statement telling you about the lien and how the owner can enforce it [[in-11]].

## What the lien covers

The owner's lien can cover [[in-11]]:

- rent, labor and other charges under the rental agreement, including late fees and rent collection costs
- expenses needed to preserve your property
- reasonable expenses of the sale or other disposal, including lien enforcement costs

**Late fee cap:** for each month you're in default, the late fee can't be more than the greater of **$20** or **20% of the monthly rent**. The owner can also recover "reasonable" costs of collecting rent and enforcing the lien [[in-115]].

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Day 5 of continuous default

The owner may begin enforcing the lien. It can deny you access to the facility and your unit, and move your things to another space [[in-12]].

### Step 2: The written notice

The owner must send you a notice by email or "verified mail" (any USPS or private delivery method that gives evidence of mailing) [[in-95]], to your last known postal or email address [[in-3]]. The notice must include [[in-12]]:

- an itemized statement of what you owe and when it became due
- a demand to pay by a date **at least 30 days after the notice was mailed**
- a statement that your unit's contents are subject to the lien, and whether you've been locked out or your property has been moved
- a name, street address and phone number to contact
- a conspicuous warning that unless you pay, your property will be advertised and sold or otherwise disposed of at a stated place and time **at least 60 days after your default**

### Step 3: Advertising

After the payment deadline passes, the owner must advertise the sale once in a newspaper of general circulation in the county, or in another commercially reasonable way likely to attract at least three independent bidders. The ad must say the property will be sold to satisfy the lien, and give the facility address, unit number (if any), your name, and the manner, time and place of sale [[in-14]].

### Step 4: The sale

It must happen **at least 10 days** after the newspaper ad or the first alternative ad [[in-14]], at the facility, the nearest suitable place, or on a publicly accessible website [[in-15]]. It must be held the way, and at the place and time, stated in your notice [[in-12]]. The owner may bid and buy [[in-15]].

### Step 5: If it doesn't sell

The owner must tell you in writing, at your last known address, what else it plans to do with the property [[in-14]].

> Vehicles, trailers and boats: instead of a sale, the owner may have them towed or removed from the facility if you don't pay by the notice deadline [[in-12]].

## How to stop the sale

Before any sale or other disposal, you can **redeem** your property by paying the owner an amount sufficient to satisfy the lien. Once you pay, the owner must return your property immediately [[in-13]]. See our step-by-step guide: [How to stop a storage auction in Indiana](/indiana/stop-storage-auction/).

## Leftover money from the sale

The owner can pay itself the lien amount from the sale. If the sale brings in more, the owner must hold the balance for you and deliver it when you ask. If you don't claim it within one year after the sale, it's treated as unclaimed property under IC 32-34-1.5 [[in-15]], which you can search for through the Indiana Attorney General's unclaimed property site [[in-unclaimed]].

## Value limits in your lease

A rental agreement may cap the value of what you can store. If it does, that cap is treated as the maximum value of your property in the unit [[in-16]]. Check this before you store anything valuable.
