We don't publish storage prices here, because they change constantly and differ by unit, floor and promotion. Instead, this guide shows how to compare honestly and keep the bill from creeping up.

## Step 1: Size down before you shop

The cheapest unit is the smallest one that fits. Before you rent, get rid of what you won't use. Our sister site Curbside Rules has guides to [heavy trash pickup, donation and drop-off in Indianapolis](https://funnyfarm4.github.io/curbside-rules/indianapolis/).

## Step 2: Compare like for like

- Price the **same size** unit at several places. Marketplaces such as SpareFoot [[sparefoot]] and Storage.com [[storagecom]] list many facilities side by side.
- Ask whether the price is for **climate-controlled** or standard, ground floor or upper floor, drive-up or interior.
- Ask about **admin fees, required locks and required insurance or protection plans**, and add them to the monthly cost.

## Step 3: Watch for teaser rates

If a facility offers a low first-month or "web special" price, find out what you'll pay after it ends. Before you sign, ask:

- What will my rent be after the special ends?
- How often has rent gone up for current tenants, and how much notice do you give?

Get the answers in writing or by email. Indiana's storage law leaves rent terms to your rental agreement [[in-16]], so what the agreement says matters. If your rent already went up, see [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)

## Step 4: Read the lien and late-fee terms

Every Indiana storage rental agreement must include a **bold** statement about the facility's lien and how it can be enforced [[in-11]]. Late fees are capped at the greater of **$20** or **20% of monthly rent** for each month in default, plus reasonable collection costs [[in-115]]. Set up autopay or a reminder: after 5 days of continuous default, a facility can lock you out and start the lien sale process [[in-12]]. See [Indiana storage auction rules](/indiana/storage-auction-rules/).

## Step 5: Check the value limit

An Indiana rental agreement can cap the value of what you store, and that cap is treated as the maximum value of your property in the unit [[in-16]]. If you're storing anything valuable, ask about it before you sign.

## Step 6: Consider peer-to-peer storage

Neighbor is a marketplace where local hosts rent out space such as garages, basements and driveways [[neighbor]]. It can work well for furniture or a car you rarely need to reach. Compare real quotes, and read the host's access rules and Neighbor's protection terms before you move anything.
