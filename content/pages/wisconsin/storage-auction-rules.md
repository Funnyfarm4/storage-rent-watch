Wisconsin's self-storage law is **Wis. Stat. 704.90, Self-service storage facilities** [[wi-90]]. In this law the renter is the **lessee**, and the **operator** is the owner, lessor or sublessor, an agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent. It also covers portable **self-service storage units** (a box, shipping container or trailer leased mainly for storage), whether kept at the company's lot or at a place you choose [[wi-90]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text, cited by subsection; your rental agreement matters too.

## The operator's lien

The operator has a **lien on all personal property stored in your space** for rent and other charges related to it, including expenses needed to preserve, remove, store, prepare for sale and sell the property. The lien attaches on the **first day** your property is stored there [[wi-90]] (sub. 3).

Your **written rental agreement** must [[wi-90]] (subs. 2m and 3):

- state **in boldface** that the operator has a lien and may satisfy it by selling your property if you default
- let you name **another person and address** the operator must also send the first notice to
- print any **limit on the value** of what you may store in bold or underlined type the same size as the rest of the agreement; that limit is presumed to be the maximum value of your property

You're in **default** when you fail to pay rent or other charges **for 7 consecutive days after the due date** in your agreement [[wi-90]] (sub. 1).

## Late fees

The operator may charge a **reasonable late fee for each month** you don't pay rent by **5 days after it's due**, but only if the amount is in your rental agreement. A late fee of **$20 or 20% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater**, is presumed reasonable. The operator can charge more, but then it **has the burden of proving** the higher fee is reasonable [[wi-90]] (sub. 4b).

## Lockouts

If you default, the operator **may deny you access** to your property until you redeem it by paying what's due [[wi-90]] (sub. 4r). The same rules apply if your rental agreement ended and you left property behind without paying storage rent: the operator may move it elsewhere or keep it where it is, charge reasonable rent for storing it, and use the same default procedure [[wi-90]] (subs. 4g and 4r).

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Default (7 days after rent is due)

Default starts when rent or other charges go unpaid **for 7 consecutive days after the due date** [[wi-90]] (sub. 1).

### Step 2: First notice

The operator must send a **first notice by regular mail or email** to your last-known address or email address, and to the person you named in your rental agreement, if any. It must include [[wi-90]] (sub. 5b):

- notice that you're **in default** (or haven't paid for property left after the agreement ended)
- notice of **denial of access**, if your agreement or the law permits it
- the **name, street address and phone number** of the operator you can contact to redeem your property

### Step 3: Second notice (at least 14 days to pay)

The operator must then send a **second notice by verified mail** (any postal or commercial mail service that provides evidence of mailing) to your last-known address, **or by email**. It must include [[wi-90]] (subs. 1 and 5b):

- a statement that the operator **has a lien** on your stored property
- notice of **denial of access**, if permitted
- an **itemized statement** of the rent and other charges due now, plus the charges that will come due before the sale and when
- a **demand for payment within a time not sooner than 14 days after the date of the notice**
- a statement that unless you pay in time, your property **may be disposed of** (if worth less than $100), **towed** (if it's a vehicle) or **sold**, with the **date, time and place** of any sale
- a statement that sale money first pays the lien, and any balance goes back to you, or to the Department of Revenue if you can't be found
- the operator's **name, street address and phone number**

When it sends the second notice, the operator must have **photos or a video** of your property available, and must show them to you **if you ask** [[wi-90]] (sub. 5c).

> **Email rules:** a notice may go by email only if the operator uses a service that **confirms receipt** and actually **gets confirmation** that the notice was received. Otherwise it must use regular or verified mail. A mailed notice is presumed delivered once it's properly addressed, stamped and deposited with the Postal Service [[wi-90]] (sub. 7). Keep your address and email current with the facility in writing; a written change-of-address notice becomes your last-known address [[wi-90]] (sub. 1am).

### Step 4: Advertising the sale

After your payment deadline passes, the operator may sell only if the sale is **advertised once in a newspaper of general circulation** where the facility or unit is located. The ad must include a brief, general description of the property and the facility's (or the portable unit operator's) address and **your name** [[wi-90]] (sub. 6a).

### Step 5: The sale

The sale can't take place **sooner than 15 days after the newspaper ad**, and it must follow the terms of the notices [[wi-90]] (sub. 6a). It must be one of these [[wi-90]] (sub. 6a):

- a **public sale** attended by **3 or more bidders**, as one lot or several
- a **private sale** after the property is offered to **at least 3 people** who deal in that type of property
- another **commercially reasonable** method, **including online**

The sale must be on an **internet site** reasonably expected to attract bidders or, if in person, at the facility, at the unit, or at the nearest suitable place. If an in-person public sale is hit by **bad weather**, the operator may postpone it **up to 14 days**, but must run a new ad explaining the postponement and send you notice of the new date, time and place **at least 5 days before** it [[wi-90]] (sub. 6am).

> **Low-value property:** if your stored property's fair market value is **less than $100**, after the notices the operator may donate it to a charity, recycle it, take it to a solid waste facility or dispose of it another reasonable way instead of selling it [[wi-90]] (sub. 5m).

> **Vehicles:** instead of selling a vehicle, the operator may have it **towed** if you were at least 7 days late, both notices were sent properly, you were **more than 60 consecutive days behind before the 2nd notice was sent**, and you didn't redeem by the deadline. The operator isn't liable for damage to or loss of the vehicle from the towing [[wi-90]] (sub. 5r).

## How to stop the sale

**At any time before** the property is disposed of, towed or sold, you may **redeem** it by paying the operator **the full amount of rent and all other charges due**. Once the operator gets that payment, it **must return your property** [[wi-90]] (sub. 5a). Ask for the itemized amount in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and get a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. The law doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a payment plan, get it in writing. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

A buyer in good faith gets your property free of your rights even if the operator broke the rules, so act **before** the sale [[wi-90]] (sub. 6c).

## Leftover money from the sale

The operator must first apply the sale money to the lien, then **return any balance to you**. If it can't locate you with due diligence, it must **report and deliver the balance to the Wisconsin secretary of revenue** under the state's unclaimed property law [[wi-90]] (sub. 6b). A Wisconsin Court of Appeals decision noted in the official statutes says nothing in the law lets the operator keep holding the extra money once you show up or contact it about it [[wi-ch704]]. Ask the facility in writing for your balance as soon as you can, and keep a copy.

If the money has already gone to the state, the **Wisconsin Department of Revenue** runs the unclaimed property program; you can search by name and file a claim online, with a copy of your government ID [[wi-up]].

## If the operator breaks the rules

- **Sue for damages:** a lessee hurt by a violation may bring a civil action for **damages, costs, disbursements and reasonable attorney fees**, plus other relief a court allows [[wi-90]] (sub. 12). The same court decision says a rental agreement can't take away reasonable attorney fees or compensatory damages under this law [[wi-ch704]].
- **Complain to the state:** the **Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection (DATCP)** investigates alleged violations [[wi-90]] (sub. 11), and you can file a consumer complaint with it online [[wi-datcp]].
- **Penalties:** a violator may have to forfeit up to **$1,000** for a first offense and up to **$3,000** for a later offense within a year; each day of a continued violation is a separate offense [[wi-90]] (sub. 10).

## Other Wisconsin rules

- **No living in units:** you may not use a storage space for residential purposes, and the operator may not knowingly allow it [[wi-90]] (sub. 2).
- **Your property, your control:** except as your agreement and the law provide, you have exclusive care, custody and control of what you store [[wi-90]] (sub. 4).
- **Rent increases:** the law doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls [[wi-90]]. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)
