Washington's self-storage law is the **Washington Self-Service Storage Facility Act**, chapter **19.150 RCW** [[wa-ch]]. In this chapter the renter is the **occupant**, and **owner** includes the operator, lessor, sublessor, agent, or anyone authorized to manage the facility or collect rent [[wa-010]]. Everything below is a plain-English summary of the statute text; your rental agreement matters too, and the chapter does not wipe out other rights the parties create so long as they don't conflict with it [[wa-ch]].

## The owner's lien

The owner has a lien on **all personal property** at the facility for rent, labor, late fees, and costs of the sale under the rental agreement, plus expenses needed to preserve, sell, or dispose of the property. The lien is enforced only as this chapter allows [[wa-020]].

A lien on a **motor vehicle or boat** that has already attached and is shown on the title documents has **priority** over the facility's chapter 19.150 lien [[wa-020]].

Your written rental agreement must say your property will be subject to a lien claim and may be sold if rent or other charges stay unpaid for **fourteen consecutive days**, and that those steps are authorized by this chapter. The agreement must also ask for, and leave space for, another person's name and address for notices. Notices go to your address and that alternative address when both are provided. Not naming an alternative contact does not cut off the owner's remedies [[wa-120]].

No occupant may use a self-service storage facility for **residential purposes** [[wa-010]]. The owner is not required to insure your property [[wa-ch]].

## Late fees and lockouts

Any late fee must be in the rental agreement (or an addendum). The owner may charge a reasonable late fee for each month you don't pay rent when due. A late fee of **$20 or 20% of the monthly rental amount, whichever is greater**, is deemed reasonable and is not treated as a penalty [[wa-150]].

After **six consecutive days** unpaid, if the rental agreement allows it, the owner may **deny access** to the space [[wa-030]].

Chapter 19.150 does **not** set a rent-increase notice period, so check your rental agreement [[wa-ch]].

## The timeline, step by step

### Step 1: Preliminary lien notice (after 14 days unpaid)

When any part of the rent or other charges stays unpaid for **fourteen consecutive days**, the owner may terminate your right to use the space by sending a **preliminary lien notice** to your last known address and to the alternative address, by **first-class mail** or by **email** if the email rules below are met [[wa-040]].

That notice must include [[wa-040]]:

- an itemized statement of the owner's claim (sums due now and when they became due)
- a statement that your right to use the space will terminate on a specified date **not less than fourteen days after the notice is sent**, unless you pay all sums due and to become due by that date
- a warning that you may be denied access after that date if you don't pay, and that an owner's lien under RCW 19.150.020 may be imposed
- the name, street address, and telephone number of the owner or designated agent you can contact

RCW 19.150.050 supplies a sample form that satisfies those requirements [[wa-050]].

**Email for this notice** is allowed only if you expressly agree, the rental agreement says in **bold type** that notices will be given by email, the owner gives you the sending address and tells you to allow it through filters, and the owner notifies you before any change to that sending address [[wa-040]].

### Step 2: Lien attaches and final lien-sale notice

If the total due is still unpaid on the date in the preliminary notice, the proposed lien **attaches that day**. The owner may then deny access, enter the space, inventory the goods, and move property to safekeeping [[wa-060]].

The owner must give you a **notice of final lien sale** (or final notice of disposition) by personal service, **verified mail**, or email to your last known address and alternative address or email. Verified mail means any USPS method that provides evidence of mailing [[wa-010]]. If the owner emails that notice and gets no reply or delivery receipt, it must send a **second notice by verified mail** to your last known postal address [[wa-060]].

The final notice must state all of the following [[wa-060]]:

- that your right to use the space has ended and you no longer have access
- that the property is subject to a lien, and the amount accrued and to accrue before the sale date
- that property other than personal papers and personal photographs may be sold after a specified date that is **not less than 14 days from the last date the final notice is sent**, or a **minimum of 42 days after unpaid rent or other charges first became due**, **whichever is later**, unless the lien amount is paid (the owner is not required to sell within any maximum number of days after charges first became due)
- that if total value in the space is **less than $300**, the owner may dispose of the property in any reasonable manner instead of selling it (subject to who may not acquire it)
- that after sale or other disposition, the owner will send you an accounting at your last known address and alternative address
- that stored vehicles, watercraft, trailers, RVs, or campers may be towed or removed instead of sold under RCW 19.150.160
- that excess proceeds over the lien and reasonable costs of sale are kept by the owner and may be reclaimed for **six months** from the sale, then turned over to the state as abandoned property under chapter 63.30 RCW
- that personal papers and photographs are kept for six months from sale or other disposition, then may be disposed of in a reasonable manner
- that you have **no right to repurchase** property sold at the lien sale

Email rules for the final notice match the preliminary-notice email rules [[wa-060]].

### Step 3: Sale or other disposition

After the time in the final notice expires, property other than personal papers and photographs may be sold or disposed of as follows [[wa-070]] [[wa-080]]:

1. **Value $300 or more:** sale in a **commercially reasonable manner** (a public sale on or off the facility, as a single lot or in parcels). If **five or more bidders** attend, the proceeds are deemed commercially reasonable [[wa-010]] [[wa-080]].
2. **Value under $300:** dispose of in a **reasonable manner** (donation to a nonprofit, removal by a trash hauler or recycler, or another method the owner reasonably chooses) [[wa-010]] [[wa-080]].

**Personal papers and photographs** that aren't reclaimed within six months after sale or other disposition may then be disposed of in a reasonable manner [[wa-080]].

**No employee or owner, and no family member of an employee or owner**, may acquire the sold or disposed property (including those papers and photographs) [[wa-080]].

Chapter 19.150 does **not** require a newspaper advertisement of the sale [[wa-ch]].

> **Vehicles and boats:** if you've been in default **60 or more days** and the stored property is a vehicle, watercraft, trailer, recreational vehicle, or camper, the owner may have it **towed** instead of sold. Before the tow, the owner must give you notice naming the towing company and its contact information. Once a third party has the property, the owner is not liable for later damage [[wa-160]].

A good-faith purchaser of goods disposed of under the $300-or-more sale rules takes free of rights of people against whom the lien was claimed, even if the owner didn't fully comply with the chapter [[wa-ch]].

## How to stop the sale

Pay the **lien amount** before the sale date in the final notice. The final notice itself says the property may be sold unless that amount is paid [[wa-060]].

Separately, **before any sale**, a person claiming a right to the property may pay the amount needed to satisfy the lien **plus one month's rent in advance**. Then the property may not be sold and must be held pending a court order on disposition. If no order arrives within **30 days**, that claimant must keep paying the monthly rent for the space; if rent stops, the owner may sell or dispose of the property again under RCW 19.150.080 [[wa-100]].

Pay in a way that leaves a record, and ask for a receipt and written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. Chapter 19.150 doesn't say a partial payment stops the sale, so if you agree on a plan, get it in writing. If the amount looks wrong, use the contact details required in the notices [[wa-040]] [[wa-060]]. If the sale date is close or you can't pay, contact free legal help (below) right away.

## Leftover money from the sale

For property sold at $300 or more, proceeds go first to **costs of the sale**, then to the **lien**. The owner keeps any **excess** for you. You (or anyone with a court order or other judicial process against the property) may claim the excess, or enough of it to cover that claim, within **six months** of the sale date [[wa-080]]. The owner may keep interest earned on excess proceeds until they are claimed or turned over to the state [[wa-080]].

After six months, unclaimed excess is turned over to the state as abandoned property under **chapter 63.30 RCW** [[wa-060]] [[wa-080]]. Search and claim later through the Washington Department of Revenue's [Unclaimed Property program](https://dor.wa.gov/about/unclaimed-property-ucp) and [claim search](https://ucp.dor.wa.gov/app/claim-search) [[wa-up]] [[wa-up-search]].

## If the owner broke the rules

Chapter 19.150 does not create a special statutory damages amount for noncompliance the way some other states do. Other rights between creditor and debtor remain available [[wa-ch]]. Keep copies of every notice and payment, and talk with free legal help if something looks wrong.

## Other Washington rules

- **No living in units:** no occupant may use a self-service storage facility for residential purposes [[wa-010]].
- **Value caps in the lease:** if your rental agreement sets a limit on the value of property you may store, that limit is the maximum value for the owner's **liability** only [[wa-ch]].
- **Rent increases:** chapter 19.150 doesn't set a rent-increase notice period, so your agreement controls [[wa-ch]]. See [My storage rent went up. What can I do?](/storage-rent-increase/)
